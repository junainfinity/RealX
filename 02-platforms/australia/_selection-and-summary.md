# Australia — Selection, Ranking & Regional Summary

Research date: 23 Sep 2026 · Region: Australia · Evidence labels: [C] confirmed (see the platform files for numbered sources) / [I] inferred / [S] speculative.

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

- **Australia has no scaled, live, retail platform issuing on-chain tokens per property as of Sep 2026.** This is based on 64 selection searches/fetches plus platform research (see `_search-log.md`).
- **The retail fractional-property market is off-chain.** It consists of registered managed investment schemes (MIS) with per-asset sub-trusts:
  - **BrickX**
  - **Assetora (ex-DomaCom)**
- **On-chain activity sits at the fund/unit/SPV level and is wholesale-first:**
  - **Tokeniser/Liquidise** on Redbelly
  - **Canvas Connect** in Project Acacia
  - These run on Australian-built infrastructure, mainly **Redbelly Network**.
- The Top 5 therefore mixes categories on purpose:
  - 2 **off-chain fractional** baselines (compliance and UX incumbents a tokenised entrant must beat).
  - 1 **infra L1**.
  - 1 **on-chain tokenisation platform** (fund/trust units).
  - 1 **regulator-backed pilot** (the only Acacia use case that tokenised a real-estate-linked asset).

**Why the off-chain incumbents are included:** they define the local legal template and UX. That template is a registered MIS with per-asset sub-trusts, an RE with an AFSL, an independent custodian holding Torrens title, TMDs, monthly distributions, a secondary order book or marketplace, and exit votes. Any tokenised Australian property product must replicate this wrapper (for retail), and on-chain rails only change the register, settlement and liquidity layers.

## 5. Regional patterns

1. **The legal wrapper is settled; the register is not.**
   - Every retail-facing Australian fractional property product uses a **registered MIS with per-asset sub-trusts or sub-funds**, an **AFSL-holding RE**, and an **independent custodian holding Torrens title**:
     - BrickX: AET + BrickX Trusco.
     - Assetora: Perpetual.
   - Tokenisation in Australia changes the **register and settlement layer, not the wrapper**.
   - The RBA's Acacia report names "enforceable on-chain ownership records" and legal settlement finality as unresolved.
2. **On-chain means wholesale (for now).**
   - Tokeniser/Liquidise (wholesale/sophisticated tests), Canvas (wholesale-only, sandbox) and JellyC (s761G) all avoid retail.
   - Wholesale thresholds were **not changed** in 2025–26 (the PJC recommended keeping A$2.5m net assets / A$250k income).
   - Retail tokenised property therefore needs a full registered MIS plus PDS and TMD.
3. **2025–26 built the regulatory scaffolding.**
   - INFO 225 (rewritten 29 Oct 2025; revised again 30 Apr 2026, adding Example 9 "83Smith": a fractional real-estate token is "likely to be an interest in a managed investment scheme"): tokenised securities and stablecoins are financial products; class relief; the sector no-action position ran to 30 Jun 2026 and has lapsed.
   - **Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026** (Act No. 38 of 2026, verified on legislation.gov.au in QA): Royal Assent 8 Apr 2026, **commences 8 Apr 2027**, 6-month transition (to 8 Oct 2027), low-value exemption (≤$10m transactions/12 months, ≤$5k per client). It creates **Tokenised Custody Platform (TCP)** and DAP licensing. The TCP definition is a **single token per asset held on trust**, so fractional property-unit tokens may sit outside TCP and remain MIS interests; custodial holding of tokens is the DAP trigger.
   - **AML/CTF tranche 2** covers real-estate professionals from **1 Jul 2026**.
   - A RealX launching in 2027 should be designed to the DAF Act's custody standards from day one, with counsel deciding early whether it is a TCP, a DAP, or neither.
4. **An Australian-built, compliance-native stack is verticalising around Redbelly.**
   - L1 (Redbelly), identity/wallet (Averer: W3C VCs + ZK proofs, embedded AA wallet) and issuance/registry (Tokeniser, with ERC-3643) are all "Redbelly Network projects".
   - AUDM is the AUD settlement coin.
   - This is the local alternative to Ethereum/Polygon + ONCHAINID stacks.
5. **Settlement money is where the regulator is focused.** Acacia tested:
   - Pilot wCBDC (about A$4.4m across 8 use cases; minted onto Redbelly and Canvas Connect).
   - Stablecoins: AUDM, AUDF, AUDD; RLUSD referenced.
   - Deposit tokens.
   - Retail property platforms still pay A$ over NPP/PayID (BrickX from Aug 2025).
6. **Liquidity is the weak point everywhere.**
   - Off-chain venues rely on internal order books with price floors and halts, or list/accept boards. They can be **frozen** (Assetora, from Jul 2024; fund re-opened Aug 2025). Exit votes **fail** (BrickX Mosman, Jun 2025).
   - On-chain players pitch AMMs and collateral/repo (Tokeniser/Liquidise, Canvas). The AMM route rests on an **untested "exempt public protocol" theory** under the s767A market definition.
7. **Tax and FIRB constraints shape the product.**
   - Resident-only eligibility and per-holder caps (BrickX 5%) keep platforms clear of FIRB and landholder duty. Assetora advertises "no stamp duty on unit transfers between members".
   - A tokenised design must encode these as transfer rules (ERC-3643 / credentials).
8. **Hype versus delivery gap.**
   - Headline "on-chain assets" figures are inflated by pipelines (Redbelly "US$73.8bn", mostly carbon credits, versus about US$1bn per RWA.io).
   - DigitalX's RWA Tokenisation Fund "failed to attract investors".
   - Hutly's US$1.8bn rent-roll tokenisation has no visible investor marketplace.
   - Assetora markets "tokenised RWAs" with no on-chain footprint.
   - Credible delivery evidence (registers, statements, votes) is scarce and valuable.

## 6. Implications for RealX (regional)

- **Wrapper:** registered MIS with per-property sub-funds (the Assetora pattern: one ARSN, many assets) or per-property trusts (the BrickX pattern). Independent custodian. TMD per asset.
- **Token layer:**
  - ERC-3643-style permissioned tokens as the register mirror, or as the register itself if the constitution allows.
  - Credential-gated transfers encoding residency/FIRB, holding caps and the wholesale/retail class.
- **Rails:** AUD via NPP/PayID for retail onboarding. AUDM/AUDD for on-chain distributions and DvP. Watch wCBDC and deposit tokens after Acacia.
- **Licensing roadmap:** RE AFSL (MIS operation, custody, dealing) now. Plan the DAF Act analysis for 8 Apr 2027 (transition to 8 Oct 2027): a TCP authorisation only if the structure is one token per asset; otherwise a DAP authorisation for custodial token holding, or reliance on the low-value exemption while small. Get a market-licence strategy or ASIC relief before any AMM/secondary venue.
- **Differentiators:** transparent on-chain register; instant A$ stablecoin distributions; continuous liquidity with NAV-anchored pricing and circuit breakers; on-chain voting that avoids BrickX-style failed exits; independent RE governance (lessons from Assetora).

- **Search budget:** the session-wide WebSearch quota (200) was exhausted during Platform 3 research. All later work used WebFetch on known URLs. Some brief names (Stax, Hitch, Kaizen, VestaBlock, PropertyChain, Openestate, Coinvest, Cirrus, Swyftx-affiliated offerings) could not be exhaustively re-checked.
- **ASIC registers:** ASIC Connect / professional registers are dynamic pages and were not fetched. AFSL numbers come from the platforms' own disclosures.
- **BrickX:** no TMD URL located; current AUM and investor count not published; legal basis for the order book (market-licence exemption) not confirmed.
- **Assetora:** PDS and fee table not retrieved; ASX quotation status in Sep 2026 is "suspended" per Market Index at fetch time; Bricklet's DLT unnamed and integration status unknown.
- **Tokeniser/Liquidise:** Tokeniser's own licensing and the legal status of the on-chain register are not disclosed; no named property-fund client; the Liquidise–Tokeniser relationship is inferred (registry redirect plus matching A$1.17–1.2B figures).
- **Canvas:** fund name and size of the real-estate credit pilot not disclosed; L2 settlement layer and token standard unknown.
- **Redbelly:** asset figures inconsistent; KYC credential issuers unnamed; the explorer page mentions "testnet", so live mainnet metrics are ambiguous.
- **Law:** DAF Act details originally came from a secondary summary (CryptoSlate); the QA pass (23 Sep 2026) verified the Act number, Royal Assent, commencement, transition, low-value exemption and TCP definition on legislation.gov.au (C2026A00038). Stamp-duty/landholder and FIRB reasoning is inferred, not confirmed by state revenue offices.
- **Assetora marketplace:** the Jul 2024 suspension is confirmed; the date the marketplace resumed is inferred (the Aug 2025 re-opening announcement covers onboarding and new sub-funds). The listed parent remained suspended with "no certainty" of reinstatement as of its Jan 2026 quarterly.
- **Redbelly explorer:** the Routescan page fetched is labelled "Testnet", so the block-time/transaction/TPS figures may not be mainnet metrics.
