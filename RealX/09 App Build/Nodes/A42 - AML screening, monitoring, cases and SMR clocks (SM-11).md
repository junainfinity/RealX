# A42 - AML screening, monitoring, cases and SMR clocks (SM-11)

**The operator-only AML engine: a screening hub that re-screens every name nightly at 03:00 and again before every payout and turns a sanctions-list update into a Sev1 within the hour; monitoring scenarios as data (rapid in and out, third-party deposits, round-tripping, structuring, unusual SMSF contributions, agent-driven order bursts); alerts that KYCA triages into MLRO cases that run the 13-state SM-11 machine under a need-to-know ACL of MLRO and CCO; restriction as freeze-now-ratify-later (`SGL+R`, the other of MLRO and CCO ratifies within 2 h, wallet freezes as A28 token operations bound to the same approval id), a restriction that stays in force while the investigation, the RFI and the SMR decision continue; SMR clocks in Australian business days counted from the day after suspicion is formed (3, or 24 elapsed hours for terrorism financing), with a 17:00 internal target and a statutory end-of-day deadline whose miss is a Sev1 and a reportable breach; TTR and IFTI items on the same rule; an RFI to the customer only after a recorded tipping-off check; exit of the relationship as MLRO to CCO; and the business-wide risk assessment and the regulatory-orders register of M20. Nothing about a case ever reaches an investor, a sponsor, support or investor relations, through a screen, an API, a token scope, a notification or a timing difference.**

**Build status:** not started. **Milestone:** AM3 (Business services). **Lane:** Compliance core. **Planner:** Opus 5.5 (high). **Builder:** Grok 4.7 (xhigh). **Node QA:** Opus 5.5 (high, every loop). **Graph QA:** Fable 5.1 (high, at milestone close).
**Estimate:** 8-12 agent-hours (build, challenge and verify loops).

**Depends on:** [[A30 - Investor onboarding, KYC cases and KYB (SM-01)]], [[A22 - Identity-DVS and sanctions sandbox adapters]], [[A17 - Bank rail sandbox adapter (NPP-PayID-PayTo) and receipt matching]], [[A28 - Token operations service (mint, burn, freeze, pause, forced transfer, recovery)]], [[A14 - Approvals engine and maker-checker (SM-00)]], [[A13 - Alerts and SLA-clocks service]]
**Unlocks:** [[A44 - Support, complaints (SM-12) and Investor 360 read model]], [[A45 - Reporting, regulatory calendar, filings and metric governance]], [[A75 - Console- AML cases, complaints register, marketing compliance, disclosure library]], [[A96 - P3- AUD stablecoin rail, V5-V6 vaults and travel rule (G15)]], [[A101 - Agent guard- anomaly detection, sensitive-action notices and tiered kill switches]]

The builder never reads that folder; the cases are published into the repo only after the gate passes. The oracle scope is fixed by the node summary and J10's oracle hooks, and it uses only interfaces that exist when this gate runs (this node's dependency closure: A30, A22, A17, A28, A14, A13 and, through them, A19, A12, A10, A09 and A06): SUPP and IRCM cannot read any case or alert field through any route of this module, any API token scope or the RLS harness, and neither can a sponsor role, a partner role or an investor; the investor-facing routes that exist at the gate (A30's `/v1/investors/me` and `/v1/investors/me/status`, A17's receipts and payout primitive, A19 simulator verdicts for the `withdrawal`, `order` and `distribution line` action kinds) and every typed outbox row this module writes carry no case data and are byte-identical (after id substitution) for a restricted and an unrestricted investor; SMR, TTR and IFTI clocks are A13 clocks counted from the day after the trigger across weekends, seeded NSW holidays and both AEST/AEDT changes, with the 17:00 internal target and the statutory end-of-day deadline as separate instants, and the 24 h terrorism-financing clock is elapsed time; a restriction is effective (G14 refuses payouts, withdrawals and orders, receipts held, wallets queued for freeze) in the same transaction as the `SGL+R` act and before any ratification, and stays in force through `investigating`, `rfi_customer` and `smr_decision` until a lift is approved; every wallet freeze is an A28 token operation carrying the restriction's approval id; no payout passes A19's G02 on a screening fact older than A19's `screening_max_age_h`; the SMR decision is a hardware-key act of the MLRO and no other principal; the SM-11 table (13 states, 16 transitions) admits nothing else; each act writes exactly the audit events listed for it in the act-to-events table below, once. Checks that need nodes built after this one (the whole-API diff over orders, payouts, distributions, activity and notifications, A44's Investor 360, A45's calendar, A101's anomaly handling, the MCP catalogue and the parity suite) are named re-runs of this node's comparer at those nodes' gates (see Gate), never faked here.

## Build brief

**Scope and placement**

- API-only: module `aml` in the Fastify monolith (`services/api/src/modules/aml/`) and the job folder `services/worker/src/jobs/screening/`, following A09's module pattern (public `index.ts` only, boundary lint, zod schemas feeding the OpenAPI document from the first commit, Drizzle schema and migration registered per module, pg-boss jobs from the worker). No screen is drawn here: ADM-25 is built by A75, the screening-hit handling on ADM-10 by A30 and A68, the block-wallet action on ADM-13 by A72 over A28, and the "account under review" flag on ADM-09 by A44. Every rule below is enforced in the API, never only in a screen.
- The only source of time is A09's injectable clock; `Date.now()` and argument-less `new Date()` are forbidden in the owned paths and grepped by `npm run verify`. Business-day arithmetic is never re-implemented here: every business-day or calendar-day clock is an A13 `sla_clock` opened with a `policy_code` (A13 applies its own `businessDaysAfter`), and the only explicit-`dueAt` clocks are regulatory orders, whose due instant is typed from the order, so the node QA's A13 calendar vectors apply to this node unchanged.
- Nothing in this module talks to a network host. Sanctions, PEP and adverse-media facts come from A22's `sanctions` adapter; bank facts from A17's intake lines; wallet facts from A28 and A27's indexer; investor state from A30; approvals from A14; alerts and clocks from A13; audit from A10; notifications as typed rows in A09's transactional outbox, whose zod schemas this module defines and tests (A43, built later, delivers them; A42 neither imports A43 nor validates template text, and stores only `template_code`, which A43 and A75 validate against A35's library at their own gates). Wallet screening (`screenWallet`) returns `not_applicable_in_phase` in P1 and P2 (A22) and is stored as such, never as `clear`; it is a neutral fact in P1 and P2 (it neither passes nor fails G02; see Screening hub) and A96 replaces it.

**Screening hub (G02)**

- Subjects come from providers, not from a hard-coded list: `registerScreeningSubjects(providerId, listActiveSubjects)` in this module's `index.ts`, where `listActiveSubjects(asOf)` returns `{subject_ref, kind (person | entity), names[], aliases[]}` rows. This node registers the investor provider over A30 (investors not `offboarded`, joint holders, entity persons and UBOs). Sponsor directors, owners and signatories are registered by the sponsor-tenancy node (A33) or a later integration node through this interface; that obligation is recorded as an upstream item (see Gate) and this node proves the interface with a fixture provider of three sponsor persons. The nightly run's `subjects_screened` is the sum over every registered provider, and the run row records `provider_counts` per provider so a missing provider is visible.
- `screenSubject(subjectRef, reason)` runs A22's `screenPerson` or `screenEntity` for the subject's current names and aliases as its provider returns them, stores an `aml_screening_result`, and, when the outcome is `review` with a hit not already dispositioned on the subject, creates one `aml_alert` (`source = screening`, dedup key subject plus list entry) and, for a sanctions-category hit, raises A13 `aml.sanctions_list_hit` Sev1 to the MLRO. PEP and adverse-media reviews create the alert without the Sev1.
- `screening.nightly` job at 03:00 `Australia/Sydney` every day (registered with the pg-boss cron here; A46 lists it as emitted by this module): every active subject of every registered provider is re-screened against the current `listVersion`; the run row records counts; a subject already under an open case is screened too and the result attaches to the case. Idempotent per date; a re-run the same day is a no-op.
- `screening.list_update` job: subscribed to A22's `ListUpdated` at boot (and re-subscribed after an adapter restart, checked by health); within 60 minutes of the event every active subject is re-screened against `addedEntryIds` and `changedEntryIds` only, and every new `review` becomes an alert plus, for sanctions, the Sev1. A22's `update-01` fixture must yield exactly one new review (the seed investor INV-00377) and exactly one Sev1. An unsubscribed handler is a Sev3 `chain.stuck_or_lagging`-class self-alert through A13 (`aml.list_subscription_lost`, an extension type registered in A13's catalogue by this node's seed test, Sev3, MLRO).
- **A19 owns the G02 and G14 verdicts; this node supplies facts only.** It registers no evaluator. It registers two fact providers with A19's `FactProvider` interface, exported from `index.ts` as pure reads with no side effect other than the refresh described next, so A19's simulator can call them:
  - `latestScreening(subjectRefs)` feeds A19's `screening_status` fact for payout kinds: per subject (the person, each owner or UBO, each wallet) `{outcome (clear | review | not_applicable_in_phase | unavailable), screened_at, list_version, screening_ref}` from the freshest `aml_screening_result`. When the freshest row is older than A19's parameter `screening_max_age_h` (A19's table 1b; one name, in hours, default 24, set with counsel and the MLRO; this node reads A19's row and defines no parameter of its own), the provider runs a synchronous `pre_payout` screen first and returns the new row; when A22 reports an outage the fact is `unavailable`. A19's G02 then fails `review` with `SCREENING_HIT_OPEN` and `unavailable` with `SCREENING_UNAVAILABLE`, and treats `not_applicable_in_phase` as neutral in P1 and P2 (neither clear nor a failure). A19 must align its own text to this (its G02 summary still says `aml.rescreen_max_age_days` and "wallet must be `clear`", and it lists no `SCREENING_UNAVAILABLE`; upstream item). A30's `screeningStatus` remains A19's source for onboarding hits; the two facts are inputs to one G02 verdict, never two evaluators.
  - `restrictionStatus(investorId)` feeds A19's `aml_restriction` fact: `none | payouts_held | full` from the active `aml_restriction` row (either origin). A19's G14 fails `G14_RESTRICTED` when the restriction blocks the kind (`full` every kind; `payouts_held` distribution lines and withdrawals, and A17's payout primitive, which asks the same question). An open sanctions case without a restriction is caught by G02 (its subject's freshest screen is `review`), so G14 has one fail condition in both specs; A19's G14 summary line ("or open Sev1 case") must be aligned to its own table (upstream item). The action kinds are A19's: deposits (A17 holds with `investor_restricted`), A17's payout primitive and `withdrawal` (A17 and, later, A39 refuse), `order` (A37 refuses), `distribution line` (A40 marks the line "AML hold, no notice" and pays nothing) and, in P2, resale orders (A91). Investor-facing surfaces render any of it only as `under_review`; the word "AML" never appears in an investor or support response body, event or template.
- The oracle proves the wiring through A19's simulator (`evaluateAction` with the real providers registered), not by calling this module's functions directly, so a divergence between the two specs fails a test at this gate.

**Monitoring scenarios (G14) as data**

- `aml_scenario` rows, versioned with `effective_from` and a `set_with_counsel` flag, each with `code`, inputs, threshold parameters, `score`, `default_severity`, `dedup_window`, `trigger_template` and `phase`. Seeded: `rapid_in_out` (a payout requested within 24 h of a credit of the same order of magnitude; A17's hint plus this node's own window over the ledger), `third_party_deposit` (A17 name mismatches; N mismatched payers within 30 days, default 3; the seed's AML-118 has 6), `structuring` (A17's hint), `round_tripping` (P1: an order funded, refunded and re-funded from a different payer within 30 days; P2 adds a buy and a sell to a related account through the resale board within the lock-in), `unusual_smsf_contribution` (deposits by an SMSF investor exceeding `smsf_annual_deposit_cap_cents` in a financial year, default a counsel-set row seeded at A$120,000, or a single deposit above `smsf_single_deposit_cents`), `agent_order_burst` (orders or resale orders placed under one API token exceeding `burst_count` in `burst_minutes` or a daily amount above the token's 30-day baseline by `burst_multiple`; the alert also emits `aml.agent_burst` for A101), `wallet_risk` (P3, A96; stored with `phase = P3` and never evaluated in P1 and P2). Adding a scenario is a row plus a seed test.
- `screening.monitor` job every 15 minutes: evaluates every scenario over new intake lines and events since the last watermark, deterministic under the injectable clock, idempotent (re-running the same window creates nothing); an alert per subject and scenario within the dedup window increments `occurrences` and refreshes evidence instead of creating a second alert. Scores and severities go on the alert; the alert never touches the investor's state.
- A `kyc.sanctions.true_match` event from A30 creates, in one transaction, an `aml_alert` (`source = kyc_true_match`, state `alert_new`, severity Sev1, the A30 approval id in `evidence_refs`) and mirrors the restriction A30's `kyc.sanctions.confirm_match` effector already applied as an `aml_restriction` row (`origin = kyc_true_match`, `kind = full`, `approval_id` = A30's, `alert_id` set, `case_id` null, `freeze_op_ids` the ops A30's freeze request produced), so G14 reads `full` from that instant. No SM-11 transition is recorded by a system actor: the alert then moves only through A03's listed actors (a KYCA triages, escalation opens the case in `case_open`, the MLRO takes it into `investigating`), and at escalation the restriction row's `case_id` is set. A `kyc_true_match` alert cannot be closed as a false positive (`AML_TRUE_MATCH_NOT_CLOSABLE`; the match was a human decision at A30), is placed at the top of the KYCA queue, and raises A13 `aml.sanctions_list_hit` Sev1 to the MLRO, so the human steps happen inside the Sev1 acknowledgement window. `suspicion_formed_at` stays null until the MLRO records it.

**Alert to triage to case (SM-11, verbatim from A03: 13 states, 16 transitions)**

- States `alert_new`, `triage`, `closed_false_positive`, `case_open`, `investigating`, `rfi_customer`, `account_restricted`, `smr_decision`, `smr_filed`, `no_file`, `post_filing_monitoring`, `exit_relationship`, `case_closed`; every transition goes through `packages/domain` `transition()` with A03's actor lists; anything else is `SM11_ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`. This node adds no state, no transition and no actor: every transition's actors are A03's list for it (the catalogue names KYCA for `alert_new -> triage`, "KYCA then MLRO" for the false-positive closure, no role for `triage -> case_open : escalated`, and MLRO for `case_open -> investigating`; this node needs A03's row for escalation to list KYCA, MLRO and CCO, checked at Specify). The alert states are `alert_new`, `triage` and `closed_false_positive` only; an escalated alert stays in `triage` with `escalated_case_id` set.
- **Restriction and case state, one model.** The live restriction is the `aml_restriction` row, never the case state. The catalogue's `account_restricted` records the restriction act inside the case: `investigating -> account_restricted` is taken by the restrict act (and only from `investigating`; a restrict attempted from any other state is 409 `SM11_ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`, and widening `payouts_held` to `full` is a new restrict act from `investigating` that supersedes the old row). `account_restricted -> investigating` is taken only by `POST /v1/aml/cases/{id}/resume` (MLRO; an explicit "resume investigation" act with a reason code) and **keeps the restriction row in force**, so a restricted case continues to `rfi_customer` and `smr_decision` without unfreezing anything; the case state never lifts or relaxes a restriction. The lift is an act on the restriction row (`POST /v1/aml/restrictions/{id}/lift`), valid while the case is in any non-terminal state; if the case is in `account_restricted` when the lift executes, the lift's effector also takes `account_restricted -> investigating` with the MLRO maker as actor. `no_file -> case_closed` and `post_filing_monitoring -> case_closed` are refused with 409 `AML_RESTRICTION_ACTIVE` while the investor has an active restriction linked to the case; only `exit_relationship -> case_closed` may close a case with a restriction still in force (the investor is `offboarded` and the restriction stays). A `kyc_true_match` restriction exists before its case and is linked at escalation; its case goes `case_open -> investigating` by the MLRO's take without passing `account_restricted`, because the restrict act happened at A30. The design's "Wallet frozen" label reads the row (see the status mapping), not the state.
- `POST /v1/aml/alerts/{id}/triage` (A03's actors for `alert_new -> triage`; the catalogue says KYCA): `alert_new -> triage`, assigns the triager. `POST .../close-false-positive` with a mandatory `closure_rationale` (at least 40 characters) and a reason code: an A14 request `aml.alert.close_false_positive` under policy `4E` (KYCA maker, MLRO checker; the catalogue's "KYCA then MLRO"; the checker must be a different human from the maker, and an agent counts as its human under A03's SoD rule 2). The policy row carries `qa_sample_rate_bps` (default 10,000, every closure checked; the MLRO may lower it to sampling by an approved policy change, in which case unsampled closures run under an `AUTO` row whose condition is `qa_sample_draw_below_rate` on A08's seeded random, and every closure, sampled or not, stays in the MLRO's QA list on ADM-25). `POST .../escalate` (A03's actors for `triage -> case_open`; reason code): `triage -> case_open`, creates the `aml_case`, the ACL, the investigation clock (A13 `queue` kind, policy `aml_investigation_5bd`, default 5 business days, owner MLRO, breach Sev3 `queue.sla_breach`), sets the alert's `escalated_case_id` (the alert stays in `triage`) and links any `kyc_true_match` restriction row to the case.
- `POST /v1/aml/cases/{id}/take` (MLRO only; CCO may reassign owner): `case_open -> investigating`, with a mandatory `suspicion` field `formed | not_yet`. `formed` sets `suspicion_formed_at` to the instant the MLRO gives (default the case-open instant; any instant up to the recording instant, never later) and opens the SMR clock (below); `not_yet` leaves the investigation clock only, and a later `POST .../record-suspicion` opens the SMR clock from the instant given. J10's oracle hook "clock computed from case open" is the default; whether the statutory clock may start later than case open is a counsel question recorded under Human gates, and the build never lets `suspicion_formed_at` exceed the recording instant.
- `POST .../notes` (ACL members; agent tokens of ACL members as maker): insert-only encrypted notes with `on_behalf_of`. `POST .../acl` (MLRO): add or remove one named KYCA analyst with a reason, audited as `aml.case.acl_changed`; the design's "Owner: Analyst" rows are cases with an analyst on the ACL, owner still the MLRO for accountability, and the read model shows the analyst as the working owner.
- `POST .../rfi` (MLRO): refused with `AML_TIPPING_OFF_CHECK_REQUIRED` unless a `aml_tipping_off_check` approved by the MLRO with a hardware-key assertion exists for this case and is younger than 24 h; then `investigating -> rfi_customer`, writes one typed outbox row (`investor.info_request` with exactly two fields, `investor_id` and `template_code`; its zod schema is tested here, and A43 delivers it later) using only the template code the tipping-off check approved (the template text is A35's and A43 and A75 validate the code against A35's library at their gates; it says that more information is needed and names documents, never a reason), sets `reply_due_at` (policy `aml_rfi_reply_30d`, calendar days), and `rfi_customer -> investigating` on reply (A44's support intake or the investor's document upload posts `POST .../rfi/{id}/reply` with a document ref) or on expiry (the MLRO decides what follows; nothing auto-closes).
- **Action codes this node needs (rows in A03's catalogue and A14's policy seeds, not code here).** The first four codes below are the product map's ADM-25 actions ("Restrict account or freeze holdings" MLRO, Ratified (`SGL+R`); "Exit relationship" MLRO → CCO; "Triage or close false positive" with SM-11's "KYCA then MLRO") and the D-section rows "Suspend account" (MLRO or CCO, `SGL+R`, optional `setAddressFrozen`) and "Freeze wallet" (MLRO/CCO sanctions path); the fifth serves the ADM-25 component "Business-wide risk assessment", which has no product-map maker-checker row, so its `4E` is this note's proposal for A03 to adopt. None exists in A03's or A14's seeded lists today, and A14 accepts `SGL+R` only for codes flagged `protective`, so without these rows `aml.account.restrict` would be refused with `APR_SGLR_NOT_PROTECTIVE`. A03 and A14 must carry them exactly as below (upstream items; the Specify step stops and A03 or A14 is reopened if they are missing, as A14 does for A03's extensions); this node records the rows it relies on, with their hashes, in `app/evidence/A42.json`:

- SMR clocks follow A13's business-day rule exactly as A13 states it: an N-business-day clock falls on the **Nth business day strictly after the local date of the trigger** (the trigger day never counts, whether or not it is a business day, and the time of day does not matter), in `AU-NSW` civil time. That matches the statute's "within 3 business days after the day" suspicion is formed, and a Saturday or public-holiday trigger therefore makes the next business day day 1, never day 0. Two instants are derived per case, both from A13, never by adding 86,400-second multiples:
  - the **statutory deadline** `smr_due_at`: the end of that local day (policy `smr_3bd`, registered by this node through A13's `registerSlaPolicies` with the due-time column at 23:59:59.999 local, `set_with_counsel`); `on_breach_type = aml.smr_clock` at Sev1, and only this breach writes `aml_reportable_breach`;
  - the **internal target** `smr_target_at`: 17:00 local on the same day (policy `smr_3bd_target`, due-time column 17:00; A13's seeded SMR row is this one if it is spelled differently, and A13's spelling wins), `set_with_counsel`; missing it is not a breach: the Sev2 `aml.smr_clock` pre-warning is re-emitted (deduped, `occurrences` 2) with `target_missed` and the CCO added as a recipient.
  Both clocks start at `started_at = suspicion_formed_at` and `subject_ref` the case. `smr_tf_24h` is one clock of 24 elapsed hours (the statutory deadline; no target). The MLRO may switch `smr_3bd` to `smr_tf_24h` (terrorism financing) with a reason, which cancels both clocks and opens the 24 h clock from the same start; the reverse is refused with `AML_CLOCK_CANNOT_LENGTHEN`. The pre-warning Sev2 `aml.smr_clock` is raised by this node's `screening.clock_watch` job (every 5 minutes) at `smr_prewarn_h` before `smr_target_at` (default 4 h, so 13:00; for `smr_tf_24h`, 4 h before the deadline), deduped per case, upgraded in place to Sev1 by A13 at the statutory breach. The case detail returns `smr_due_at`, `smr_target_at`, `smr_remaining_ms` (to the deadline), `smr_target_remaining_ms`, `smr_policy`, `target_missed: boolean` and `late: boolean` so ADM-25 shows the clock without computing anything. Whether counsel wants a margin inside the statutory day (for example AUSTRAC Online cut-off times) is a data row, not code.

## Node-specific challenges (node QA)

- **Tipping off by a side channel (compliance, security).** Two seeded investors with identical profiles; one is restricted under `SGL+R` at 09:00 with `full` freeze. Over the next clock hour the node QA reads, as each investor and as their agent tokens, every investor-facing route that exists at this gate (A30's `/v1/investors/me` and `/v1/investors/me/status`, A17's receipt and payout-primitive responses, A19 simulator verdict bodies for `withdrawal`, `order` and `distribution line`), then every typed outbox row this module wrote in the hour, then the latency per route with the statistic defined under Need-to-know. Any byte other than ids differing, any status code differing, a held receipt labelled anything but "under review", a verdict body carrying a reason other than the generic hold, an outbox row mentioning a hold, a case or AML, or a latency test failing its threshold fails the node. The same comparer is re-run later at A44 and A81 over the routes those nodes add.
- **Restriction that waits for a signature (money).** The MLRO acts `aml.account.restrict` with `full`; before any V3 signer signs the freeze and before the CCO ratifies, the node QA asks A19's simulator for a withdrawal, a distribution line and a new order for that investor, calls A17's payout primitive, and posts a PayID credit through A17's sandbox: the withdrawal, the distribution line and the order must fail G14 with `G14_RESTRICTED`, the payout primitive must refuse, the credit must be held `investor_restricted`, and all five in the same second as the act. Then nobody ratifies for 2 h: exactly one Sev2 `approval.unratified`, escalation on A13's ladder, the restriction still in force, the freeze op still pending or confirmed, no `AUTO` lift request. Then the MLRO resumes, sends an RFI with a valid check and reaches `smr_decision`: no `unfreeze` op exists, G14 still fails, and the label still reads "Wallet frozen". A restriction that takes effect only at ratification or at chain confirmation, that lifts on lapse, or that is relaxed by `resume`, fails.
- **Clock arithmetic through the weekend and the DST change (compliance).** Under A13's rule (the Nth business day strictly after the local date of the trigger; target 17:00 local, statutory deadline the end of that local day) a case taken with suspicion formed at 15:00 on Saturday 3 October 2026 must have `smr_target_at` at 17:00 on Thursday 8 October 2026 AEDT (06:00 UTC) and `smr_due_at` at the end of Thursday 8 October AEDT (12:59:59.999 UTC): Monday 5 October is Labour Day in NSW, so Tuesday 6 is day 1, Wednesday 7 day 2 and Thursday 8 day 3, and DST began on Sunday 4 October. A case taken at 10:00 on Thursday 1 April 2027 has day 1 Friday 2 April, day 2 Monday 5 April and day 3 Tuesday 6 April, so the target is 17:00 AEST (07:00 UTC) on Tuesday 6 April 2027, after the DST end on Sunday 4 April (Easter 2027 falls on 26 to 29 March, so no holiday intervenes). A TTR item for an NPP credit at 20:00 on Saturday 3 October 2026 is due at the end of Monday 19 October 2026 AEDT (10 business days counted from Tuesday 6 October). A `smr_tf_24h` case taken at 23:30 AEST on Saturday 3 October 2026 (13:30 UTC) is due at 13:30 UTC on Sunday 4 October, which reads 00:30 on Monday 5 October AEDT, not 23:30 Sunday, because that local day has 23 hours. A pre-warning must fire at exactly `target - 4 h` within one clock-watch tick, the target-missed re-emit at the target, and a Sev1 at the statutory deadline within one A13 detector tick, once each. Any implementation that adds 86,400-second multiples, uses the server zone, counts the trigger day, or re-implements A13's calendar fails at least one vector.
- **The SMR decision by everyone but the MLRO (security, authority).** The CCO with a valid hardware-key assertion, the MLRO's own API token holding every M20 scope, an `AGNT` principal on behalf of the MLRO, `SVCA`, and a break-glass session each call `smr-decision` and `file`: every call is refused with `AML_MLRO_ONLY` or `APR_CHECKER_MUST_BE_HUMAN` before any scope check, the case stays `investigating`, and each token attempt writes one audit event and one typed `agent.sensitive_action` outbox row (A101 consumes it at its own gate). Then the MLRO files at 17:01 on the due day: the filing is on time with `target_missed = true`, no `aml_reportable_breach` exists, and the Sev2 target-missed alert reached the MLRO and CCO. A second case filed at 00:00:01 the next local day is `late`: one `aml_reportable_breach` dated by the statutory deadline and one Sev1 `aml.smr_clock` to the MLRO and CCO. A decision recorded for any other principal, a breach recorded at 17:01, or a late filing recorded as on time, fails.
- **Need-to-know through the back doors (security).** A KYCA analyst removed from a case's ACL an hour ago, a SUPP user with a token minted before their role changed, a sponsor owner whose investor appears on the case, TP-AUD reading `aml_reportable_breach`, and an ADM-31 audit reader who is not on the ACL: each attempt to read the case, its notes, its filings or its audit events must return 404 or the redacted "AML · restricted event" row with no subject; a database-level probe through A04's RLS test harness as `app.principal_role = SUPP` must return zero rows from `aml_case`, `aml_alert`, `aml_restriction`, `aml_risk_assessment` and `aml_regulatory_order`. A cached read model, an export or an audit payload leaking one field fails.
- **Design fidelity and a list update at scale (design, compliance).** With the standard seed, `GET /v1/aml/cases` must reproduce the four `adAml` rows field by field (aliases, investor ids, trigger text, "19 Sep" style dates, owners, labels and tones), `summary.open_cases` must be 4, and A13's summary must still read "3 (1 high)"; then `trigger('list-update', { fromFixture: 'update-01' })` at 10:00 must, by 11:00 clock time, yield exactly one new `review`, one new alert on INV-00377 attached to the existing AML-118 case (no second case), one Sev1 `aml.sanctions_list_hit`, and one `aml_screening_run` of kind `list_update` whose `subjects_screened` equals the active subject count of every registered provider, including the three fixture sponsor persons. Two alerts, a second case, a missing Sev1 or a nightly-run-sized screen fails.
