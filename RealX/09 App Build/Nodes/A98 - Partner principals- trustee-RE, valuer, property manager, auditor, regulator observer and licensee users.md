# A98 - Partner principals: trustee/RE, valuer, property manager, auditor, regulator observer and licensee users

**The six licensed-partner user types who sign in, turned into real principals: `partner_user` rows of principal type PARTNER, each bound to a verified A33 entity; onboarding through a `4E` request (PART maker, CCO checker) with KYB, licence and panel checks; identity verification, passkey sign-in, a 30-day dormancy lock and quarterly access reviews as for staff; scoping by engagement (vehicles, properties, tenants and a time box) enforced by row-level security; valuers and property managers as limited sponsor-console members (SP-VAL uploads valuations, SP-PM uploads rent rolls and property reports), which closes product-map QA item 6; the trustee or RE (TP-RE) as the named checker the approval policies already call "the trustee", only for its own vehicles and never through an agent; the auditor and the regulator observer as read-only, time-boxed users and the licensee as a read-only user with no time box; the registry sandbox's trustee acknowledgement made an act of a real TP-RE person; and every partner action callable by the partner's own agent under a token that can never exceed the partner's own cells.**

**Build status:** not started. **Milestone:** AM3 (Business services). **Lane:** Services. **Planner:** Opus 5.5 (high). **Builder:** Grok 4.7 (xhigh). **Node QA:** Opus 5.5 (high, every loop). **Graph QA:** Fable 5.1 (high, at milestone close).
**Estimate:** 10-14 agent-hours (build, challenge and verify loops). Raised from 6-10 by the 24 Sep 2026 plan review: now an oracle node, with the checker-eligibility matrix for every approval policy row and every partner kind.

**Depends on:** [[A11 - Authentication, sessions, RBAC and segregation of duties]], [[A12 - API tokens, scopes, agent principals and mandates for every user type (G22)]], [[A33 - Sponsor tenancy, entities, licences of sponsors and admission pipeline]], [[A18 - Unit register (legal record) and registry sandbox adapter]], [[A34 - Properties, valuations and proof of property (SM-03)]], [[A35 - Documents, disclosure library, e-sign and acknowledgements (G12)]]
**Unlocks:** [[A45 - Reporting, regulatory calendar, filings and metric governance]], [[A62 - Sponsor console- apply, dashboard, team and approvals, billing]], [[A82 - End-to-end- an agent drives the account through the API and MCP]], [[A105 - Partner portal- trustee-RE, auditor, regulator observer and licensee views]]

**Oracle rule:** this is an oracle node (`oracle: yes`, set on 24 Sep 2026 by the plan fixer after the plan review). No money, supply or holdings move here, but authority does: this node decides who may act as "the trustee" on approvals that move units and money (exit distributions, forced transfers, recoveries, `register.adjust`), which is exactly the case D04 asks oracles for. The [[App Gauntlet]] §2 rules for oracle nodes therefore apply to A98 in full, with no exception: the node QA writes hidden tests from this frozen spec to `<workspace>\gauntlet\hidden\A98\` before the build starts (the scoping property test, the checker-eligibility matrix for every approval policy row and every partner kind, the token-ceiling sweep, the lifecycle transition oracle, the time-box and engagement-end vectors, and the same-person probes); the builder never reads that folder, the Oracle step cannot be skipped or shortened, and the tests are published into `app/` only after the gate passes. The frontmatter carries `oracle: yes`, so the graph and the orchestration schedule the Oracle stage at max effort.

What this node is for in one sentence: the product map names eleven licensed partners and providers, six of them sign in, and until this node nobody owned those six as principals, so "the trustee countersigns" in A18, A28, A40 and A48 had no real person behind it. This node gives them one. It draws no screen: SPN-14 and SPN-16 for valuers and property managers are drawn by A62 and A60, the partner portal for TP-RE, TP-AUD, TP-OBS and TP-LIC by A105, the data room by A45 and A105. This plan is not legal advice; every sentence about what counsel, a trust deed or a regulator requires is a counsel question.

## Build brief

This node is API-only: module `partners` in the Fastify monolith (`services/api/src/modules/partners/`, worker jobs registered from the same folder through A09's job registry). Every rule below is enforced in the API, never only in a screen; every route is in the OpenAPI document from the first commit so `packages/sdk` and the MCP tool catalogue (A12, A104) see it before the module passes. The only source of time is A09's injectable clock (`Date.now()` and argument-less `new Date()` are grepped by `npm run verify`).

**1. What this node reuses and what it adds (no duplication of A03, A04, A11, A12, A14, A18, A30, A33, A34, A35)**

- The principal type and the rule set come from A11: principal type `PARTNER`, the kinds TP-RE, TP-VAL, TP-PM, TP-AUD, TP-OBS, TP-LIC (A03's `principals.ts` marks these six `signsIn: true`; TP-CUS, TP-REG, TP-KYC, TP-BANK and TP-SIGN stay adapter counterparties with no cells, and an attempt to onboard one of them as a user is refused with `PARTNER_KIND_NOT_SIGN_IN`), the scope shape "assigned vehicles / engagement / time box", A11's verify-email then passkey invitation path, its 30-day dormancy job (partners unlocked by PART or SECO), `requiresHuman`, `requireFreshAssertion`, `eligibleCheckers`, and the `RoleAssignment` table with `expires_at` that feeds `GET /iam/roles/review-set`.
- Cells come from A03's role x module x action matrix, one row per partner kind: TP-AUD and TP-OBS read and export cells only, all time-boxed; TP-LIC read cells on M21 and M23 only; TP-VAL `valuations:submit` and `documents:write` for `valuation_report` drafts; TP-PM `rent_roll:import`, attestation items `rent_roll`, `maintenance_log`, `arrears_vacancies` and `documents:write` for `property_report` drafts; TP-RE read on M04, M09, M11, M18 and M22 for its vehicles, `register:export`, `envelopes:manage` for register certifications, the A14 decide cell on the policy rows that name the trustee, `exit:propose`, `winddown:step` and the trustee acknowledgement in §5. This node invents no cell; if a row is missing it is requested from A03's owner at Specify.
- Role labels versus matrix rows: **SP-VAL and SP-PM are only A33 membership and invitation role labels** (the value in A33's invitation `role` enum and in `partner_engagement.role` for sponsor-console members). They map one to one onto the A03 matrix rows **TP-VAL** and **TP-PM**, which are the only rows any cell check reads: A11 authorises an SP-VAL member against the TP-VAL row and an SP-PM member against the TP-PM row, and A34's valuer and attestation checks already name TP-VAL and TP-PM. A03's `roles.ts` gains no role (it keeps exactly its 51 roles and its test that rejects any other id); the mapping `{SP-VAL: TP-VAL, SP-PM: TP-PM}` is a constant in this module's `index.ts`, and a structural test asserts that no cell, policy row or token scope anywhere names SP-VAL or SP-PM as a role id.
- Tables: `partner_user` is A04's (`id` `PTR-`, `principal_id`, `person_id`, `kind`, `entity_id`, `state`, `display_name_enc`, `email_bidx`, `time_box_ends_at`, `hardware_key_registered`, `dormant_since`). This node registers one table through A04's module-schema registry, `partner_engagement` (below), with `tenant_id`, row-level security and A03 enums exactly as a core table, or `db:check` fails. The RLS session variable `app.vehicle_scope` already exists in A04 ("partner sessions see only their assigned vehicles or engagements"); this node is what fills it.
- Entities come from A33: every partner user is bound to one `entity` (`trustee` or `responsible_entity` for TP-RE, `valuer_firm` with `panel_valuer = true` for TP-VAL, `property_manager_firm` for TP-PM, `licensee` for TP-LIC). Auditors and regulators have no entity type yet: this node asks A33's owner for two new enum values in A03's data, `audit_firm` and `regulator` (spec point (b)). Firm KYB runs through A33's existing path (which reuses A30's `kyb/`); each person runs A30's person KYC.
- Approvals come from A14 through the policy rows A03 already carries (`register.certify` 4E REGO → trustee; `register.adjust` 6E REGO → CCO + CFO + trustee; `token.forced_transfer` and `token.recovery` 6E+Q with the trustee where the row says so; `distribution.exit.approve` 6E FINA → CFO + LEGL + trustee countersign; `property.propose_exit` TP-RE or ASTM → CCO; `property.approve_exit` with the trustee as checker on the wholesale track; `winddown.trustee_control` TP-RE → CCO). This node does not decide anything on those requests; it supplies the checker-set resolver that turns "trustee" into eligible people (§5), through hooks that A11 and A14 do not have yet and that are requested as spec point (f), and it adds two action codes of its own: `partner.onboard` and `partner.engagement.widen` (both `4E`, PART maker, CCO checker, the product map's "Onboard partner: KYB plus licence check, PART → CCO" row on ADM-29).
- Tokens come from A12 unchanged: `rx_ptr_` for TP-RE, TP-AUD, TP-OBS, TP-LIC and `rx_spn_` for the SP-VAL and SP-PM sponsor-console members; A12's `partner` issue path already caps expiry at the partner's time box and limits cells to the partner's own. This node supplies the ceiling A12 reads (`partnerScope`, §8) and the events that end tokens.

**2. The six partner kinds: what each is bound to and where each signs in**

- **TP-RE, trustee or responsible entity.** Bound to a `trustee` or `responsible_entity` entity. Scope is derived, never typed in: the vehicles whose A33 `vehicle.trustee_entity_id` equals the partner's entity, recomputed on every request, so a trustee change on a vehicle moves the scope at once and no hand-kept list can drift. Signs in to the partner portal (A105). In P1 this is each sponsor's licensed trustee (the design's Harbour & Co "acts as trustee under its own AFSL" and Yarra Bend Partners' "hired professional RE"); in P2 it is the hired RE of the umbrella scheme, bound to every sub-fund.
- **TP-VAL, independent valuer, and TP-PM, property manager.** Bound to a `valuer_firm` or `property_manager_firm` entity, which is onboarded once at platform level. Each sponsor that engages the firm adds a tenant membership with the limited role **SP-VAL** or **SP-PM** (new values of A33's invitation `role` enum, as A33 anticipates) and an engagement listing the vehicles and properties. They sign in to the **sponsor console**, not the partner portal: this is the resolution of product-map QA item 6 ("valuers and property managers are limited sponsor-console users in P1"), and it is why A62 lists them on SPN-14 and A60 gives them SPN-16. Proposed model (spec point (a)): one PARTNER principal per person per engaged tenant, linked by `person_id`, exactly as A33 links an investor and a sponsor user of the same person, so tenant context never mixes; the session resolves to that sponsor tenant and `app.vehicle_scope` narrows it to the engaged vehicles.
- **TP-AUD, auditor.** Bound to an `audit_firm` entity. Read and export only, time-boxed; scope is an engagement (tenants, vehicles, date range) plus any A45 data-room grant. Signs in to the partner portal. Attesting a metric as audited is A45's person-only act for TP-AUD; this node only makes the auditor a real principal A45 can check.
- **TP-OBS, regulator observer.** Bound to a `regulator` entity (ASIC, AUSTRAC). Read only, time-boxed, and in P1 has no scope at all outside an A45 data-room grant: a TP-OBS with no active grant is a principal with no endpoints except `GET /v1/partners/me`.
- **TP-LIC, licensee.** Bound to the `licensee` entity whose AFSL RealX operates under as a CAR (A21's licence register). Read only on M21 (marketing items and rulesets through A43) and M23 (complaints) for the platform tenant and the licensee's own calendar rows in A45; no vehicle scope. **Not time-boxed:** its standing engagement has no `ends_at` (the relationship lasts as long as the CAR appointment; it ends through `partner.offboard` or the licence events in §3), `time_box_label` is `null` and A50's grey pill is not shown. TP-RE is not time-boxed either (its scope is derived from the vehicles, §5). Only TP-AUD and TP-OBS carry a time box and the pill.
- **Grants come through an interface, not from A45 directly.** A45 owns `data_room_grant` but depends on this node, so no real grant exists when A98 reaches its gate. This node therefore defines `GrantSource` in `scope/grant-source.ts`: `listActiveGrants(principalId, at)` returning `{grant_id, principal_id, collections[], starts_at, ends_at, state}` rows, and `subscribe(onGrantChanged)` for the change callback. A45 registers the real implementation at start-up (`registerGrantSource`, exported from `index.ts`); until it does, the scope hook treats the partner as having no grants (fail closed: a TP-OBS has no scope). A fixture implementation `FixtureGrantSource` (grants inserted and revoked by the test, times on the injectable clock) is loaded only when the `test` profile is active; A09's profile guard makes it impossible to register in any other profile, and a structural test asserts that. All of A98's grant behaviour (grant-scoped reads, grant expiry and revocation, TP-OBS agent parity, the token cap at the grant end) is tested against the fixture; the same tests re-run against the real A45 effector, `reporting.dataroom_sweep` and SECO's revoke as A45's challenges, which already depend on A98.

**3. Onboarding, identity and the partner lifecycle**

- Firm first: the partner's entity must exist in A33 with `kyb_status = approved` (G03) before any person can be onboarded (`PARTNER_ENTITY_NOT_VERIFIED`). For TP-RE the entity must hold a `verified` trustee licence or AFSL row in A33 (`PARTNER_LICENCE_NOT_VERIFIED`); for TP-VAL the firm must be `panel_valuer = true` with a current registration (`PARTNER_NOT_PANEL`); for TP-LIC the entity must be the licensee named in A21's register (read through A33's `licencesForEntity`, since A21 is not a dependency). For a `regulator` entity KYB does not apply; verification is an official request letter on file (A35 document, `kind = regulator_request`) and the partner's government email domain, a proposed rule recorded under HG-LEGAL.
- `POST /v1/admin/partners/onboard` (PART, or PART's agent as maker) with `{entity_id, kind, email, display_name, engagement}` drafts the A14 request `partner.onboard` (`4E`, PART maker, CCO checker). Its precheck, registered here through `registerPrecheck`, runs the firm checks above, refuses an empty scope (`PARTNER_SCOPE_EMPTY`), refuses a TP-AUD or TP-OBS engagement without an end date (`PARTNER_TIME_BOX_REQUIRED`) or longer than the configured maximum (`PARTNER_TIME_BOX_TOO_LONG`; defaults in `packages/config`, set with counsel: 365 days for an audit engagement, 90 days for an observer), and refuses a second live `partner_user` for the same person, kind and entity (`PARTNER_EXISTS`). The effector creates `partner_user` in `invited`, the first `partner_engagement` in `pending`, and an invitation through A11's path (single-use 32-byte token, 7-day expiry, at most 3 resends).
- SP-VAL and SP-PM members are requested by the sponsor: an active SP-OWN calls A33's `POST /v1/sponsor/team/invitations` with role SP-VAL or SP-PM, a firm id and the vehicles; A33 calls the validator this node registers for those two roles (A33 is upstream and cannot import A98, so A33 gains `registerInvitationValidator(role, fn)` and `registerTeamRowsProvider(fn)` in the follow-up of spec point (g); this node registers `validatePartnerInvitation` and `teamPartnerRows` through them at start-up, and A33 refuses SP-VAL and SP-PM invitations, fail closed, while no validator is registered). The validator requires the firm to be an onboarded, active partner entity of the right type and, for a valuer, independent of the tenant (below). The engagement is created by the SP-OWN (tenant-internal, single actor, audited) because the firm itself was already admitted by PART and CCO; widening an engagement to another tenant's vehicle is impossible by RLS (`PARTNER_SCOPE_OUTSIDE_TENANT`).
- Independence (G20): a valuer firm may not be engaged on a vehicle whose sponsor, trustee or custodian entity is the firm itself or is a related party of the firm (`PARTNER_NOT_INDEPENDENT`). A33 has no related-party model, so **this node owns one**: the table `partner_related_party` (`PRP-<6 base32>`: `firm_entity_id`, `related_entity_id`, `relationship` (`common_director`, `common_owner`, `parent_or_subsidiary`, `fee_dependence`, `other`), `source_doc_id` (A35), `recorded_by`, `recorded_at`, `ended_at`), registered through A04's module-schema registry with RLS, edited only by PART, CCO or VALO (single actor, audited, never in place: an edit inserts and closes). Every insert or end emits `partner.related_party.changed`, whose handler, in the same transaction, re-runs the independence check on every active engagement of the firm, moves each engagement that now fails to `suspended_independence` (a state of `partner_engagement`, scope removed at once as for `ended`), writes one audit event per engagement and places a `valuation_independence_hold` flag on every draft or submitted valuation the firm uploaded for the affected vehicles, which A34 surfaces to VALO through its existing review queue (the flag is read by A34 through `partnerScope`'s `independence_holds[]`; if A34 cannot read it, that is an A34 follow-up request, never patched here). The engagement check at creation and every re-check read the same table. What counts as a related party is a counsel question under HG-LEGAL; the relationship codes are data. This is spec point (g). The engagement stores the firm's independence declaration document (A35, `kind = valuer_independence`); whether that declaration is personal to the valuer is a counsel question, so the upload route is `requiresHuman` until answered (the row is `"counsel"` in A03's `delegable.ts`).
- Acceptance: the invitee accepts through A11's verify-email then passkey path (`requiresHuman`), which moves `partner_user` to `pending_identity` and opens an A30 person KYC case (the same case type as a sponsor user; a partner is never exempt, the one exception being the regulator path above). `kyc.case.approved` plus at least one `active` engagement moves the partner to `active`. Until `active` the principal's effective cells are empty except `GET /v1/partners/me` (`PARTNER_NOT_ACTIVE` on anything else).
- TP-RE hardware key: a TP-RE may be a checker only with a hardware-attested passkey, because A14's `decide` verifies decisions through A11's hardware-attested verifier. `hardware_key_registered` is set when the TP-RE registers a passkey whose AAGUID is on A11's allow-list; until then the resolver in §5 leaves the person out and `GET /v1/partners/me` returns `can_countersign: false` with the reason `PARTNER_HARDWARE_KEY_REQUIRED`, so A105 can say what is missing. Whether a trustee may decide with a platform (software) passkey instead is a counsel and SECO question recorded under HG-LEGAL; the default is the stricter rule.
- Lifecycle of `partner_user`, a small local transition table in A03's engine format (not one of the 14 product-map machines): `invited -> pending_identity -> active`; `invited -> expired` (invitation lapsed); `active -> dormant` (A11's 30-day job) `-> active` (unlock by PART or SECO with a reason code); `active -> lapsed` when the last engagement ends or its time box passes, `lapsed -> active` on a new approved engagement; `active | dormant | lapsed -> suspended` (manual: `partner.suspend`, `SGL+R`: PART, SECO or CCO acts with a hardware-key assertion, a different person among CCO or SECO ratifies within 2 h; automatic: the upstream events below), `suspended -> active` (`partner.reinstate`, `4E`, PART → CCO; the only way out of `suspended`); any non-terminal state `-> offboarded` (terminal; `partner.offboard`, PART or SECO single actor with a reason; also automatic when the entity is struck off or its licence expires). Every transition writes exactly one audit event; a transition not in the table is refused with `PARTNER_SM_ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`.
- Suspension never fails open. The `partner.suspend` row that this node proposes to A03 is a protective `SGL+R` row that is **not** `reversible` (A14 auto-reverts only rows marked `reversible`), so a missed ratification raises A14's ratification-lapsed alert, escalated as Sev2 to the CCO through A13's interface stub, and the partner stays `suspended` until a `partner.reinstate` `4E` passes; a rejected ratification is also not a revert (it opens the reinstate path with the ratifier's reason code, still `4E`). The automatic suspensions (licence expired, entity reverification, KYC refresh failure, related-party re-check in §3) are system transitions made by the service principal `SVCA` with a mandatory `trigger_ref` to the upstream event, not A14 requests at all, so there is nothing to ratify and nothing that can revert; they too end only through `partner.reinstate`. Tests: a manual suspension with no ratification after 2 h + 1 s on the fake clock leaves the partner `suspended`, its sessions and tokens revoked, and one Sev2 alert; each automatic suspension leaves no A14 request and survives any clock advance.
- Fail closed on everything upstream: `tenancy.tenant.suspended` (A33) ends every SP-VAL and SP-PM engagement in that tenant; `entities.entity.reverification_required` suspends every partner of the entity until KYB passes again; a licence moving to `expired` in A33 moves every TP-RE of that entity to `suspended` and raises Sev2 to CCO through A13's interface stub; `investor`-style KYC refresh failure (A30 `refresh_due -> suspended`) suspends the partner. In each case, in the same transaction, this node revokes the partner's sessions (A11) and emits the events A12 uses to revoke tokens.

**4. Engagements and scoping (row-level security, never a filter in a handler)**

- `partner_engagement` (`PEN-<6 base32>`): `partner_user_id`, `tenant_id` (the sponsor for SP-VAL and SP-PM; the platform tenant for TP-AUD, TP-OBS, TP-LIC), `role` (TP-RE, SP-VAL, SP-PM, TP-AUD, TP-OBS, TP-LIC), `vehicle_ids[]`, `property_ids[]` (derived for TP-RE and never stored; explicit for the others), `collections[]` (TP-AUD only, the A45 collection codes an audit engagement may read outside a data-room grant), `starts_at`, `ends_at` (required for TP-AUD and TP-OBS; null for TP-RE and TP-LIC; optional for SP-VAL and SP-PM), `state` (`pending`, `active`, `suspended_independence`, `ended`, `expired`; only `active` gives scope), `approval_id`, `independence_doc_id` (valuers), `created_by`, `ended_reason`. Never edited in place: a widening inserts a new row under `partner.engagement.widen` (`4E`) and closes the old one; a narrowing or an end is a single-actor act by PART, SECO, CCO or, for its own tenant's SP-VAL and SP-PM engagements, the SP-OWN.
- Each active engagement is also written as an A11 `RoleAssignment` with `expires_at = ends_at`, so the quarterly access review (A67) lists every partner grant beside staff roles through the existing `GET /iam/roles/review-set`, with no second review source.
- Session scope: at every request (not only at sign-in), the partner middleware registered with A11's session hook reads the principal's active engagements and the derived trustee vehicles inside the request transaction and sets `SET LOCAL app.tenant_id` and `app.vehicle_scope`; A04's policies (both `USING` and `WITH CHECK`) then decide every read and write. Nothing is cached across requests, so an engagement ended or expired at 10:00:00 refuses a request at 10:00:01 on any API process. - Precedence of refusals, fixed so a correct build and a wrong one can be told apart: (1) a principal that is not `active` gets 403 `PARTNER_NOT_ACTIVE` (or `PARTNER_SUSPENDED`, `ACCOUNT_DORMANT`); (2) an active principal whose resolved scope is **empty** (no active engagement, no derived trustee vehicle, no active grant) gets 403 `PARTNER_NO_SCOPE` before the handler on every route except `GET /v1/partners/me`; (3) an active principal with a **non-empty** scope asking for an id outside it gets 404, never 403, so a partner cannot learn that another vehicle, tenant or grant exists (the same rule as A18, A33 and A45). The empty-scope 403 reveals nothing about any other object, because it is returned whatever id is asked for, including ids that do not exist.
- Data-room scope: A45 owns `data_room_grant` and reaches this node through the `GrantSource` interface (§2). This node exports `onGrantChanged(grant)`, which the registered grant source calls (the real A45 grant effector and `reporting.dataroom_sweep` in production, `FixtureGrantSource` in the test profile); on `revoked` or `expired` it drops the grant's collections from the principal's scope in the same transaction and emits `partner.scope.narrowed` so A12 recomputes token rights on the next call. A grant may name only an `active` partner of kind TP-AUD, TP-OBS or TP-LIC (A45's `DR_GRANTEE_NOT_PARTNER` and `DR_PRINCIPAL_INACTIVE` read `partnerScope`).
- What no partner ever sees, whatever the scope: AML flags and case ids, freeze reasons (a frozen holder shows "Frozen" as on the design's `spRegister` row, nothing more), sealed filings (A45), need-to-know audit events (A10's ACL), another partner's engagement, staff notes on the partner's own onboarding. PII is masked through A04's `mask()` exactly as A18 and A34 define for the kind; this node adds no unmask right.

**5. The trustee or RE: a real checker, only for its own vehicles**

- The hooks this needs do not exist yet (spec point (f), settled before the hidden tests are written). A11's `eligibleCheckers(request)` has no way to register a resolver, and A14's policy rows carry fixed `checker_role_sets[][]` whose `applies_when` selects a stricter row only on amount or variance thresholds, never on a vehicle attribute. This node therefore requests one A11/A14 follow-up loop, run before its build starts and never patched here: (1) **A11** adds `registerCheckerSetResolver(name, fn)` and calls the registered resolver from `eligibleCheckers` when a checker set in a row is a named set rather than a role list; an unregistered name resolves to the empty set; (2) **A03** policy rows name the set `TRUSTEE_OF_VEHICLE` in their `checker_role_sets` on `register.certify`, `register.adjust`, `token.forced_transfer` and `token.recovery` (where the product map names the trustee), `distribution.exit.approve`, `property.approve_exit` (wholesale track) and `winddown.trustee_control`, plus P2's meeting-result certification row used by A93; (3) **A14** adds a row-selector predicate hook, `registerRowPredicate(name, fn)`, usable in `applies_when`, and A03 adds a second `distribution.run.approve` variant that carries a `TRUSTEE_OF_VEHICLE` stage and whose `applies_when` is the predicate `vehicle_trustee_countersign_all_runs`, which this node registers. Until the loop lands, A14 resolves the name to A11's fixture PARTNER principal, the same interim A12 uses.
- Checker resolution: the registered `TRUSTEE_OF_VEHICLE` resolver, given an approval request, returns the `active`, non-dormant TP-RE principals bound to the entity that is `trustee_entity_id` of the request's vehicle, with `hardware_key_registered = true`, minus A14's maker identity set and minus the same-person exclusions below. It is only ever called for a row that names the set, so a TP-RE is never a checker outside the rows the policy names, and never for another trustee's vehicle. **An empty trustee set on a row that names the trustee fails closed:** the stage is never skipped, dropped or satisfied by anyone else; A14 raises `approval.no_eligible_checker` and the request waits (or expires on its TTL) until an eligible TP-RE exists. A test asserts that no row naming the trustee can reach `approved` with a trustee stage unfilled.
- Same-person rule, enforced twice. When the trustee entity is the sponsor's own company (Harbour & Co in the design), the TP-RE principal may belong to a person who is also SP-OWN of that tenant. (i) At resolution, the resolver excludes any TP-RE whose `person_id` equals the `person_id` of the maker or of any human who has already decided on the request. (ii) Because A14 resolves `required_checkers[]` once at submit and later stages may be decided in any order, this node also registers a **decide-time precheck** with A14 (`registerDecidePrecheck`, part of the same follow-up (f); if A14 prefers, an equivalent segregation-of-duties rule in A11 keyed on `person_id`): any `decide`, `veto` or `ratify` whose decider's `person_id` equals the maker's person or the person of any earlier decider on the same request, in any capacity and whatever the stage order, is refused with 409 **`PARTNER_SAME_PERSON`**, a refusal code in the OpenAPI document, not only an audit note. An agent counts as its human and a partner principal counts as its person. If exclusion leaves nobody, A14 raises its `approval.no_eligible_checker` Sev3 as for any empty set. Whether a sponsor that is its own trustee needs a separate trustee countersign at all is A40's counsel question, repeated under HG-LEGAL here.
- Ordinary distribution runs: A40 names the trustee only on exit runs. A per-vehicle data flag `trustee_countersign_all_runs` (default `false`, a `set_with_counsel` row in A03's data, spec point (d)) selects the `distribution.run.approve` variant with the `TRUSTEE_OF_VEHICLE` stage for that vehicle when counsel or the trust deed requires it, through the row predicate of point (f)(3). This node ships the flag's reader, the predicate and the resolver; A40 already routes its runs through A14, so no A40 code changes, but A11, A14 and A03 do change through point (f).
- Certification: A18's `register.certify` checker is the vehicle's TP-RE through this resolver. A18 also names TP-REG as an alternative checker; TP-REG does not sign in (A03), so its part is the adapter's `certifyVersion` record, not a human decision. The version's `certified_by` is the TP-RE principal id from the A14 decision.
- The registry sandbox's trustee acknowledgement (spec point (c)): on the wholesale track some trustees keep their own register, which A18 models as the same adapter interface. For a vehicle whose `register_keeper = trustee`, an instruction submitted by A18 is acknowledged only when the vehicle's TP-RE calls `POST /v1/partners/trustee/instructions/{id}/acknowledge` or `/reject` with a fresh passkey assertion (A11, purpose `trustee_ack`, over the instruction's canonical hash and `registry_ref`). This node records the act (principal, assertion id, instruction hash, decision, reason code on a rejection), writes `partner.trustee.acknowledged` to A10, and then drives A18's sandbox acknowledgement through A18's `index.ts` with the principal and assertion ids attached, so the ack A18 applies carries a real checker identity instead of an anonymous sandbox actor. The route is `requiresHuman` (an execute act; agents never hold `O`). An acknowledgement over a different hash is `FRESH_ASSERTION_MISMATCH`; one for another trustee's vehicle is 404; a second one is a no-op. If A18's `index.ts` exposes no hook to drive its sandbox ack with an actor, that is requested as A18's follow-up loop before this node's build starts, never patched here. The product map's J04 step "Trustee issues units on the register" (ADM-17) is this same route: on a vehicle whose `register_keeper = trustee`, the issuance instruction A18 submits for a primary allotment is acknowledged only by the vehicle's TP-RE through `/acknowledge`, exactly as any other instruction.

This node ships only the resolver, registered at runtime, so A93 needs no import of A98 (A93 does not depend on A98; if A93 is built first its tests use A14's interim fixture PARTNER principal). - Reads for the trustee: its own vehicles' register entries, cap table, versions and signed extracts (A18 routes, scoped by `app.vehicle_scope`), token-ops read-only views (A28), offering and halt reads (A36, A48), documents for its vehicles (A35), and the approvals awaiting it, `GET /v1/partners/me/approvals?stage=your_turn`, a scoped view over A14's inbox that returns only requests where this principal is in `required_checkers[]` now. **6. Valuers and property managers inside the sponsor console (G20, G19)**

- SP-VAL can: list the properties and vehicles of its engagements; upload a valuation draft through A34's `POST /v1/properties/{id}/valuations` (A34 sets `valuer_user_id` and checks `valuer_entity_id` is a panel firm; this node guarantees that the uploader's firm is that entity and that the property is in scope); attach the report through A35 (`valuation_report` drafts); read the valuations it uploaded (A34 already limits a valuer to its own uploads). SP-VAL cannot submit, review, publish or approve a valuation, see another valuer's valuations, or see the sponsor's cash, debt and liabilities entries; it holds no `A` cell anywhere, so SPN-14 shows "–" in "Can approve".
- SP-PM can: import rent rolls (`rent_roll:import`), fill the attestation items `rent_roll`, `maintenance_log` and `arrears_vacancies` with evidence (A34 `PUT /v1/attestations/{id}/items/{kind}`), attach `property_report` drafts (A35), and see its own tasks (A43 `audience = partner`, `assignee_role = SP-PM`; A43 is not an ancestor of this node, so at A98's gate this is checked only as the scope contract A43 will read, `partnerScope(...).property_ids[]`, and the real task delivery is checked in A65's J14 run). SP-PM cannot fill any other item (insurance, rates, land tax), sign the attestation (a human SP-OPS or SP-OWN with a passkey, A34), or read the register, distributions or investor data.
- Both roles: read the tenant's listings only for engaged properties, receive tasks and notices only for engaged properties, and appear on SPN-14 in the group "Partners working in this console" with the firm name and the limited role label the API returns ("Valuer · uploads valuations", "Property manager · uploads rent rolls and reports"). `GET /v1/sponsor/team` (A33) composes these rows through this node's `teamPartnerRows(tenantId)`, and `invitable_roles[]` includes SP-VAL and SP-PM only for an SP-OWN and only when at least one onboarded firm of that type exists.

**8. Agents and tokens (D04, G22)**

- Every partner kind may issue tokens through A12 for its own agents, with the same self-service rules as anyone else: a fresh passkey assertion to create or widen; SP-VAL and SP-PM tokens (`rx_spn_`) wait in `pending` until an SP-OWN of the tenant approves (A12's sponsor path); TP-RE, TP-AUD, TP-OBS and TP-LIC tokens (`rx_ptr_`) activate on the partner's own assertion.
- The ceiling is `partnerScope(principalId)`, exported from `index.ts`: `{kind, entity_id, tenant_id, vehicle_ids[], property_ids[], collections[], grant_ids[], time_box_ends_at, read_only, can_countersign}`. A12 intersects the token's scopes with the partner's live cells and this scope on every call; `expires_at` of a TP-AUD or TP-OBS token is capped at the earliest `ends_at` of its engagements and grants (a TP-LIC or TP-RE token, having no time box, takes A12's default maximum expiry, and any grant-scoped right it holds still ends with the grant through `onGrantChanged`); a token names no vehicle outside the scope (`G22_VEHICLE_NOT_ALLOWED`); a read-only partner's token carries no write family at all (`SCOPE_EXCEEDS_OWNER` at issue).
- What a partner's agent may do (delegable): the reads above; a valuer's agent uploads a valuation draft and its report; a property manager's agent imports a rent roll and fills its three items; a trustee's agent reads registers, issues a signed register extract (A18's single-actor extract, `register:export`) and lists the approvals awaiting its human; an auditor's or observer's agent reads within the engagement or grant; a licensee's agent reads marketing and complaints. Each call is audited "agent <token prefix> on behalf of <partner>" and appears in the partner's activity.
- What a partner's agent may never do (refused before scope evaluation): accept an invitation, register a passkey or do identity verification (`AGNT_NOT_ALLOWED`); create or widen a token; decide on any A14 request, including every trustee countersign and veto (`APR_CHECKER_MUST_BE_HUMAN`, `G22_AGENT_NEVER_CHECKER`); acknowledge or reject a registry instruction as trustee (`AGNT_NOT_ALLOWED`); upload the valuer independence declaration while counsel has not answered (`SCOPE_PENDING_COUNSEL`); attest a metric (A45 `MET_ATTEST_PERSON_ONLY`).
- Staff side: a PART's agent may draft `partner.onboard`, `partner.engagement.widen`, narrowings and offboarding requests as maker within its human's role; the CCO checker step is refused to every token by A14; segregation of duties counts the agent as its human, so a PART whose agent drafted the onboarding cannot be the checker (PART never holds the checker cell anyway) and a CCO whose agent drafted a widening cannot approve it.
- Events that end or narrow tokens, consumed by A12's automatic revocation: `partner.engagement.ended`, `partner.engagement.expired`, `partner.scope.narrowed`, `partner.suspended`, `partner.offboarded`, `partner.dormant`. Ending one engagement of several narrows the token on the next request (A12 recomputes rights live); ending the last one, suspension and offboarding revoke every token of the principal in the same transaction.

## Node-specific challenges (node QA)

- **The trustee of the wrong trust (money, G13).** Yarra Bend's hired RE (TP-RE bound to the RE entity) tries to decide on a fixture `distribution.exit.approve` request for a Harbour & Co vehicle (A40 is not an ancestor; the real exit run is A80's), on a `register.adjust` for a Northline vehicle, and on a withdrawal over A$50,000 (a row that names no trustee): all three must leave it out of `required_checkers[]` and A14's `decide` must refuse it; then the vehicle's `trustee_entity_id` is changed to the RE entity through A33 and the very next resolution includes it, while a request drafted before the change keeps its original checker set (A11's rule that later eligibility never applies to earlier drafts).
- **One person, two hats (G13).** The Harbour & Co trustee director (Mia Chen) is also SP-OWN. On a fixture `distribution.run.approve` request for a vehicle with `trustee_countersign_all_runs = true`: (i) they approve as SP-OWN before submit resolution, and the resolver must exclude them from the trustee stage; (ii) the order is reversed, with the trustee stage resolved at submit while they were still eligible, and they decide as TP-RE first, then try the SP-OWN stage (and, in a third run, the other way round): the second decision must be refused at decide time with 409 `PARTNER_SAME_PERSON`, whatever the stage order. The request must then wait for another eligible TP-RE or raise `approval.no_eligible_checker`, never skip the trustee stage, and their own agent token acting for either principal must be refused as checker. Any request where one `person_id` appears in two decisions fails the node.
- **The engagement that ends mid-upload (security, G20).** A valuer's agent streams a 20 MB report upload while the SP-OWN ends the engagement on the other API process; the upload transaction that commits after the end must be refused by RLS (`WITH CHECK`) and leave no valuation row, no document row and no stored object reference; a second call a second later gets 404 on the property; the valuer's token is narrowed (another engagement remains) rather than revoked; the draft started before the end stays readable by SP-FIN.
- **Independence through a side door (compliance, G20).** A valuer firm that is recorded in this node's `partner_related_party` table as a related party of Northline is engaged by Northline directly, then through a new person of the same firm, then a second, unrelated firm is engaged and afterwards a `partner_related_party` row linking it to Northline is inserted: the first two must be refused `PARTNER_NOT_INDEPENDENT`; the third must, through `partner.related_party.changed` in the same transaction, move the engagement to `suspended_independence`, drop its scope on the next request, and place `valuation_independence_hold` on any draft or submitted valuation the firm uploaded so it reaches VALO flagged rather than reach review silently.
- **Observer after the bell (security).** Using `FixtureGrantSource` (the real-grant version is A45's challenge), a TP-OBS holds grant G1, 1 to 30 October 2026 Sydney time, and grant G2 over other collections, open to 30 November: a read of a G1 collection at 23:59:59 on 30 October succeeds; at 00:00:01 on 31 October a read of a G1 collection by the person and by its agent both fail with the same 404 before the sweep runs (scope still non-empty because of G2); the token's `expires_at` equals the earliest grant end; then G2 is revoked at 10:00:00 and a read at 10:00:01 on either process, of any id, gets 403 `PARTNER_NO_SCOPE` (scope now empty), while `GET /v1/partners/me` still answers; the observer then tries a write route with a forged scope claim in the body and gets `FORBIDDEN` before the handler.
- **The anonymous trustee comes back (security).** A registry instruction on a `register_keeper = trustee` vehicle is acknowledged (i) through A18's sandbox test hook with no actor, (ii) by the TP-RE's agent token, (iii) by the TP-RE with an assertion over another instruction's hash, (iv) by the TP-RE of another vehicle, (v) correctly: only (v) may change the register, and its A18 ack record must carry the TP-RE principal id and assertion id; (i) must be impossible outside the test profile, (ii) `AGNT_NOT_ALLOWED`, (iii) `FRESH_ASSERTION_MISMATCH`, (iv) 404.
- **A partner token wider than its partner (agent misuse, G22).** An auditor with an engagement over two Harbour & Co vehicles requests a token with `register:instruct`, with a third vehicle, and with an expiry past its time box; then its engagement is widened by a `4E` and its existing token tries the new vehicle. Expected: `SCOPE_EXCEEDS_OWNER` naming the cell, `G22_VEHICLE_NOT_ALLOWED`, expiry refused or capped per A12's rule, and the old token gaining nothing until the human widens it with a fresh assertion.
- **Design fidelity of the data contract.** With the standard seed, `GET /v1/partners/me` for each of the six kinds and `teamPartnerRows` for Harbour & Co must produce the exact A50 card strings ("Trustee and RE", "Auditor", "Regulator observer", "Licensee", "Read-only · time-boxed to 31 Dec 2026" for the auditor and the observer only, `time_box_label: null` for the trustee and the licensee), the frozen organisation strings "Meridian Fiduciary Services" and "Carrow Licensing Services Pty Ltd" (never a sponsor name for the licensee), and the SPN-14 labels ("Valuer · uploads valuations", "Property manager · uploads rent rolls and reports", "–" in Can approve); any new state string, any bare 4xx or any field the A62, A105 or A77 contract tests need that is missing fails.
