# A16 - Client-money ledger (double entry)

**The single book of record for every cent of AUD that RealX holds for other people: a double-entry ledger in integer cents, kept wholly in the RealX platform tenant, with one cash account per investor, one escrow account per offering with a per-order sub-ledger, a mirror account per client-money bank account, fee and rounding accounts, and an operating account that is marked "not client money" and never counted. Every journal balances inside one database transaction, every approval-bound journal is tied to one A14 approval by object, amount and policy strength, available and reserved balances are exact, withdrawal thresholds are A14 policy rows (never local config), statement-cut snapshots are reproducible, and the wallet and activity read contracts are published to the SDK for the investor and staff screens and their agents.**

**Build status:** not started. **Milestone:** AM1 (Money, register, approvals and agent-access core). **Lane:** Money. **Planner:** Opus 5.5 (high). **Builder:** Grok 4.7 (xhigh). **Node QA:** Opus 5.5 (high, every loop). **Graph QA:** Fable 5.1 (high, at milestone close).
**Estimate:** 12-16 agent-hours (build, challenge and verify loops).

**Depends on:** [[A09 - API and worker skeleton with OpenAPI-generated SDK]], [[A02 - Domain ids, money and unit math]], [[A10 - Append-only hash-chained audit log]], [[A13 - Alerts and SLA-clocks service]], [[A14 - Approvals engine and maker-checker (SM-00)]], [[A06 - Seed data and scenario simulator]] (A12's token principals arrive transitively through A14; `Data\app-graph.json` carries these edges and is the authoritative graph file)
**Unlocks:** [[A17 - Bank rail sandbox adapter (NPP-PayID-PayTo) and receipt matching]], [[A20 - Three-way reconciliation engine with zero tolerance]], [[A37 - Orders, escrow, allocation and refunds (SM-05)]], [[A39 - Treasury- withdrawals, escrow release, client-money reconciliation]], [[A40 - Distributions engine (SM-08)]], [[A47 - Portfolio, holdings and transactions read models with statements]]

**Oracle rule:** The node QA writes hidden oracle tests from this spec to `<workspace>\gauntlet\hidden\A16\` before the build starts ([[App Gauntlet]] §2). The builder never reads that folder. The oracle scope is the posting engine and its invariants: property tests that no cent is created or destroyed across 100,000 random journal sequences; that reserved never exceeds balance and available is never negative; that the client-money zero-sum holds per currency across the platform ledger after every journal; that the per-order escrow sub-balances always sum to the `offer_escrow` balance; that the operating account is excluded from every client-money total; that every approval-bound journal type refuses to post without an approval that matches it by object, action code, amount (line by line against the approved file for batch rows), the policy A14 resolved at submit, state and the applicable A14 clock, and that a single-object approval id and a file line are never used twice; that the same external reference never posts twice; that `order.fund` and `distribution.pay` post for a sponsor vehicle; and that a statement-cut snapshot is reproducible from the entries alone.

## Build brief

This node is API-only (module `ledger` in the Fastify monolith plus two worker jobs). It has no screens of its own: POR-05 (AUD wallet), POR-04 (activity), POR-07 (statements) and ADM-18 (treasury) are built by A47, A39 and their owning UI nodes on top of the read contracts below. The ledger never talks to a bank: A17 posts bank facts into it, A37, A39, A40, A41 and A42 post business facts into it, and A20 reads it. Every rule below is enforced in the service layer, never only in a screen or a caller.

**Tenancy: one platform ledger**

- Every ledger row (accounts, journals, entries, reservations, snapshots) carries `tenant_id` = the RealX platform tenant, because RealX (as the arranger holding client money on trust) is the one party that owes every client-money liability and holds every client-money bank account. A sponsor tenant never owns a ledger row. This satisfies A04's deferred trigger (every entry's `tenant_id` equals its journal's) for every catalogue row, including those that touch a sponsor vehicle's `offer_escrow`, `distribution_payable`, `withholding_payable` or `rounding` account.
- Accounts that belong to a sponsor's vehicle or offering carry `sponsor_tenant_id`, `vehicle_id` and `offering_id` as plain attributes. They are used only to scope reads: A37, A40, A47 and A64 (all downstream of this node in the graph) read a sponsor's own figures through this module's `index.ts` with a `vehicle_id` filter checked against the caller's own-vehicles constraint. Sponsor sessions and sponsor tokens never read the ledger tables directly (under A04's RLS the platform-tenant rows are invisible to them), and this node publishes no sponsor route.

**Chart of accounts (`ledger_account`)**

- Columns: `id`, `tenant_id` (platform, by check constraint), `account_type`, `currency` (`AUD` only at launch; any other value is refused with `LDG_CURRENCY_UNSUPPORTED`; the column exists so P3 stablecoins are a data change), `side` (`asset` or `liability`; a client-money asset is a bank mirror, a client-money liability is what RealX owes a person, an offering or a trust), `is_client_money` (boolean, derived from `account_type` by a check constraint and not settable by callers), `owner_ref` (investor id, offering id, vehicle id, bank account ref or `platform`), `sponsor_tenant_id`, `vehicle_id`, `offering_id` (attributes, above), `external_ref` (masked bank account such as `••2201` for mirrors), `status` (`open`, `frozen`, `closed`), `frozen_approval_id`, plus the standard `created_*`/`updated_*` columns.
- Account types and cardinality, all created lazily and idempotently by `ensureAccount(type, owner_ref)`:
  - `investor_cash` — one per investor (liability, client money). The design's "Funds available" is its available balance.
  - `offer_escrow` — one per offering (liability, client money), with a **per-order sub-ledger**: every leg on it carries `line_ref` = the order id and `beneficiary_id` = the investor whose money it is (the one exception is the `settlement:<offering id>` line, beneficiary the vehicle, which holds a trustee settlement the bank failed or returned until `escrow.settlement_resend`). An order's escrow sub-balance is `Σ credits − Σ debits` of the legs with that `line_ref`; it may never go negative. The design's "Awaiting investment · In offer escrow" is the investor's Σ of sub-balances across offerings; ADM-18's "Offer escrow" row is the bank-side mirror.
  - `distribution_payable` — one per distribution run (liability, client money), funded by the sponsor's payment and drained by the per-holder lines (A40's step table).
  - `distribution_carry` — one per vehicle (liability, client money) for the dust owed to holders between runs (A40's carries).
  - `withholding_payable` — one per vehicle per financial year (liability, client money until remitted; A41 computes and approves the remittance, this node posts `withholding.remit`).
  - `suspense_unmatched` — one per client bank account (liability, client money) for receipts A17 cannot match or that wait for the manual-match approval; every leg carries `line_ref = bank_transaction:<id>`; ages for A13's "unmatched PayID receipt older than 48 h" alert.
  - `payout_clearing` — one per client bank account (liability, client money) for money sent to a bank but not yet confirmed. Every credit to it carries `line_ref = payout:<payout id>` and writes one `ledger_payout` row (`payout_id`, `purpose` = `withdrawal` | `suspense_return` | `escrow_settlement` | `distribution_bank` | `withholding_remit` | `rounding_sweep`, `source_account_id`, `source_line_ref`, `beneficiary_id`, `amount_cents`, `state` = `open` | `settled` | `failed` | `returned`). A settle, failure or return names the payout and consumes its row; a failure or return credits the money back to the exact source account and line it came from, so a failed trustee settlement lands back in the offering's escrow (on the line `settlement:<offering id>`, beneficiary the vehicle), a failed ATO remittance back in `withholding_payable`, and so on. Invariant: `payout_clearing` balance = Σ `open` payout rows, per account.
  - `shortfall_receivable` — one per client bank account (asset, client money) recording money the bank holds less of than the ledger says (a bank fee or error debited from the trust account), owed to the client pool by RealX until topped up from operating.
  - `fee_payable_realx` — one (liability, client money until swept) holding the 0.5 % transaction fee and the platform fee **only once earned**: an order's fee stays inside its `offer_escrow` sub-balance until `escrow.release`, so the account never holds a fee that a refund could still need.
  - `rounding` — one per vehicle (liability, client money) for distribution remainders (A40's `distribution.residual`); when it exceeds `ledger.rounding.sweep_cap_cents` (module config, default 10,000 cents; a housekeeping trigger, not an approval threshold) A13 raises `ledger.rounding_over_cap` to TRSY, who drafts the `rounding.sweep` journal below.
  - `float_interest_payable` — one per client bank account (liability, client money) for interest the bank credits on the trust account; disclosed on PUB-06 and treated per the counsel question in Human gates.
  - `bank_mirror` — one per client-money bank account (asset, client money): "Client money · wallets", "Offer escrow", "Distributions" in the design.
  - `operating` — one per RealX operating bank account (asset, **not** client money) and `revenue` (its liability-side pair, not client money). Both carry `is_client_money = false` by constraint; the design labels them "Fees (not client money) · Separate".
- Invariant per currency across the platform ledger, checked after every journal in the same transaction and by the nightly job: `Σ client-money asset balances − Σ client-money liability balances = 0`, and, separately, `Σ operating − Σ revenue = 0`. The per-line invariants are checked alongside: for every offering, `Σ line sub-balances = offer_escrow balance` (order lines plus any `settlement:<offering id>` line); for every `payout_clearing` account, balance = Σ `open` payout rows. These are the ledger's own integrity checks; the bank-versus-ledger Δ against A17's statements belongs to A20 (rule R10).

**Journals and entries (`ledger_journal`, `ledger_entry`)**

- A journal is immutable once posted: `id`, `tenant_id` (platform), `journal_type` (from the catalogue below), `external_ref` (unique per `journal_type`; a bank transaction id, an order id, a distribution line id, a withdrawal id, an offering id), `approval_id` (nullable; a check constraint plus the catalogue row decide when it is mandatory), `origin` (below), `effective_date`, `posted_at` (from A09's injectable clock, never `new Date()`), `posting_seq` (a gapless monotonic sequence across the platform ledger, the ordering key for snapshots and cursors), `memo` (free text with PII detection warnings, never bank details), `reversal_of` and `reversed_by` (the only column that may change after insert, set once by a column-restricted trigger), `lines[]`.
- Each entry: `journal_id`, `account_id`, `side` (`debit`/`credit`), `amount_cents` (`bigint`, strictly positive), `line_ref` (the order, line or withdrawal the leg belongs to; mandatory on every `offer_escrow` leg), `beneficiary_id` (mandatory on every `offer_escrow` leg), `balance_after_cents` (the account's balance after this leg, in `posting_seq` order). A journal needs at least two entries; `Σ debits = Σ credits` is checked in code before insert and again by A04's deferred constraint trigger at commit; a journal that fails either is refused with `LDG_UNBALANCED` and nothing is written. All arithmetic goes through A02's `Cents` functions; a JavaScript `number` on any money path fails the lint from A01.
- Escrow leg rules: in `order.fund` the `offer_escrow` credit's `beneficiary_id` must equal the owner of the `investor_cash` account debited in the same journal; in `order.refund` the `investor_cash` credited must belong to the line's `beneficiary_id`; no journal may take an order's sub-balance below zero. A breach is refused with `LDG_ESCROW_LINE_MISMATCH`.
- **Origin (agent provenance, D04).** `postJournal` and `reserve` take `origin = { principal_kind: 'HUMAN' | 'PARTNER' | 'AGNT' | 'SVCA', principal_id, token_id?, on_behalf_of_id? }`, which is never trusted as a free caller field: a synchronous post takes it from A09's request context `ctx` (principal, `token_id`, `on_behalf_of_id` as A11 and A12 set them); a post made later by a worker takes it, for an approval-bound row, from the approval request (A14's `maker_principal`, or the payload's `originated_by` block where the maker is a service such as "System threshold") and, for a row without an approval, from the triggering outbox event's `actor_kind`, `actor_id`, `token_id` and `on_behalf_of_id` (A09). A command whose origin differs from that source is refused with `LDG_ORIGIN_MISMATCH`; an origin with a `token_id` and no `on_behalf_of_id` is refused with `LDG_ORIGIN_INVALID` before any write (A04's audit CHECK would otherwise reject the event). The origin is stored on the journal and on the reservation, copied to the audit event (`token_id`, `on_behalf_of_id`) and returned by `me/ledger`.
- Posting is one transaction: `postJournal(cmd)` locks the touched accounts with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` in ascending account id order (deadlock-free), validates the catalogue row, account status, approval binding, origin, currency and balance rules, inserts journal and entries, updates the balances, writes exactly one audit event through A10's writer in the same transaction with `actor_id`, `token_id`, `on_behalf_of_id`, `approval_id` and the journal hash, and emits one outbox event `ledger.journal.posted` (A09) for the callers' state machines. A crash between any two of those steps leaves nothing behind.
- Idempotency: `postJournal` takes the caller's `Idempotency-Key` (A09) and additionally enforces `UNIQUE (tenant_id, journal_type, external_ref)`; a second post with the same reference returns the original journal with `replayed = true` and posts nothing. A second post with the same reference and a different amount is refused with `LDG_EXTERNAL_REF_CONFLICT`.
- Corrections are contra journals only: `reverseJournal(id, reason_code, approval_id)` posts a `reversal` with the legs swapped, links both rows, and refuses when the reversal would take any account's available balance or any escrow sub-balance below zero (`LDG_REVERSAL_WOULD_OVERDRAW`) or when the original is already reversed (`LDG_ALREADY_REVERSED`). There is no update or delete path on journals or entries beyond `reversed_by`; the API role has no such grants and A04's triggers reject them.

**Balances, reservations and holds**

- Per account the module keeps `balance_cents` (posted), and derives `reserved_cents = Σ active reservations` and `available_cents = balance_cents − reserved_cents`. Invariants enforced on every write: `0 ≤ reserved ≤ balance` for liability accounts, `available ≥ 0`, asset mirrors never negative; a debit journal that would breach them is refused with `LDG_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE`, a reservation that would with `LDG_RESERVE_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE`.
- `ledger_reservation` row: `account_id`, `amount_cents`, `purpose` (`order`, `withdrawal`, `aml_hold`), `purpose_ref`, `origin`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (orders: the offering's close plus `ledger.reservation.order_grace_hours`; withdrawals: `ledger.withdrawal.reservation_ttl_hours`; holds: none), `state` (`active`, `consumed`, `released`, `expired`). `reserve`, `release` and `consume` (which posts the paired journal and closes the reservation in the same transaction) are the only operations. An `aml_hold` is placed only by an explicit `reserve(…, 'aml_hold', …)` from A42 bound to its restriction's approval id; the ledger never places one on its own. The worker job `ledger.reservation_sweep` (every 5 minutes) expires stale reservations and emits `ledger.reservation.expired` so A37 can move the order to `expired`.
- Freeze: `freezeAccount(id, approval_id)` and `unfreezeAccount(id, approval_id)` require an approval id bound to the account or its owning investor (A42 raises them under `SGL+R` for AML restrictions, `4E+Q` to lift; action codes in the catalogue below). A frozen `investor_cash` account still accepts credits (deposits and distributions land) but refuses every business debit and every new reservation with `LDG_ACCOUNT_FROZEN`. The error carries no reason; the investor-facing read model shows only `under_review` (tipping-off).
- Bank facts on a frozen account: a debit the bank has already made (`deposit.returned`, a recall of money already credited) is posted, never refused, because the money has left the trust account whatever the ledger says; the journal is flagged `posted_while_frozen`, the available balance may not go below zero (the shortfall is parked as `reversal_pending` by A17 exactly as for an unfrozen account), and A13 raises `ledger.frozen_account_bank_debit` to MLRO and TRSY only (no investor notice).

Bank-originated undo uses its own rows (`deposit.returned`, `deposit.unmatched_returned`, `*.failed`, `*.return(ed)`), never `reversal` | the owning service of the original |
| freeze / unfreeze (not journals) | none | `aml.account.restrict` · `SGL+R` (A42), or A14's `aml.sanctions_freeze` · `SGL+R`; lift: `aml.restriction.lift` · `4E+Q` (A42) | the account or its owning investor | A42 |

- A journal whose legs do not match its catalogue row (wrong account types, wrong count, a missing `line_ref` or `beneficiary_id` on an escrow leg, a missing `payout:<id>` line on a `payout_clearing` leg, a non-client account outside the three two-sided rows) is refused with `LDG_LEGS_NOT_IN_CATALOGUE`; a non-client account mixed into any other row is refused with `LDG_CLIENT_MONEY_BOUNDARY`. The only rows allowed to touch `operating` or `revenue` together with client accounts are `fee.sweep`, `interest.sweep` (operating variant) and `shortfall.topup`, and each side of each must balance on its own.
- **Approval binding (the ledger's share of G13; A14 owns who may approve).** For every row that names an approval, `postJournal` reads the approval through A14's `index.ts` inside the posting transaction and refuses with `LDG_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` when there is none, or `LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH` unless all of the following hold:
  1. **Object.** The approval's `object_ref` equals the row's approval object from the table above, and its `action_code` is the row's A14 action code.
  2. **State and clock (A14's two clocks).** The approval's state is one of `approved`, `queued_for_custody`, `signed`, `broadcast`, `confirmed` (A03's spelling wins). In `approved` (not yet accepted for execution) the decision TTL applies: `expires_at` must be after the posting instant, whether or not A14's expiry sweep has run. In `queued_for_custody` the `execution_deadline` applies instead. In `signed`, `broadcast` and `confirmed` the approval has been accepted for execution and no TTL applies, so a `fee.sweep` or `bank.internal_transfer` confirmed by the bank days later, or the fifth chunk of a refund or distribution batch, still posts. `queued` (timelock not elapsed), `reconciled`, `closed` and every terminal state (`rejected`, `expired`, `cancelled`, `failed`) are refused. `reconciled` cannot legitimately come first, because for every approval-bound row the ledger journal is the A14 postcondition: this node publishes `journalPostcondition(approval_id)` (true once the single-object journal has posted, or once every line of the approved file is consumed for batch rows), and the owning effectors (A37, A39, A40, A41) register it with A14's `registerEffector`.
  3. **Amount.** Single-object rows: the amount the row defines equals the `amount_cents` in the approval's `payload_canonical` (a payload without it is a mismatch). Batch rows use the approved-file binding below.
  4. **Policy strength, as resolved at submit.** (a) The approval's `policy_code` is at least the row's floor, on the order `AUTO` < `4E` < `4E+Q` < `6E` < `6E+Q` (`SGL+R` is accepted only on freeze rows); nothing but `6E` or stricter releases escrow. (b) The ledger re-runs A14's resolver in replay mode over the approval's own `policy_version`, its frozen `payload_canonical` and the fact snapshot A14 stored at submit (for withdrawals: `destination_allowlisted` and `same_day_sum_cents`), never over live facts, and requires the replayed code to equal the stored `policy_code`. A `4E` approval therefore cannot pay a withdrawal whose frozen amount selects the second-approval row, while a later same-day request, a Sydney midnight or a later `policy.change` never re-tiers or strands an approval already granted. A stricter policy applies to requests submitted after it takes effect; cancelling in-flight requests after a policy change is A14's decision, never the ledger's.
  5. **Single use.** For single-object rows the approval id is consumed by exactly one journal (a partial `UNIQUE (approval_id)` on those types); a second journal naming it is refused even with a different `external_ref`.
- **Approved-file binding (batch rows: `order.refund` batch and revert, `distribution.carry_release`, `distribution.residual`, `distribution.pay`, `distribution.pay_bank`).** Before the first journal under a batch approval the caller registers the file once with `registerApprovedFile(approval_id, canonical_file)`: the ledger recomputes `sha256` over the canonical bytes and requires it to equal the approval payload's `file_sha256` (A37's refund batch file, A40's payment file), else `LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`, and stores one `ledger_approved_file_line` per file line (`approval_id`, `line_id`, `beneficiary_id`, `amount_cents` for refunds; `net_cents`, `withholding_cents`, `carry_in_cents`, `carry_out_cents`, `exception_code` for distributions; `consumed_journal_id`). The line count, the approved total and, for a run, the funded gross come from the file, never from the caller; where the payload also carries `total_cents` it must equal Σ lines. Each line journal must name its `line_id`, and its beneficiary and every leg amount (net, withholding, refund) must equal that line exactly; each line is consumed once (`UNIQUE (approval_id, line_id)`, a replay with the same `external_ref` returns the original); a journal for a holder not in the file, a pair of lines with money moved between them at an unchanged total, or a changed withholding split is refused with `LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`. `ledger_approval_draw (approval_id, approved_total_cents, drawn_cents)`, locked `FOR UPDATE` in the same transaction, keeps `drawn ≤ approved total` across chunks. When the last line of a run's file is consumed, Σ of the run's `distribution.residual`, `distribution.pay` and `distribution.pay_bank` postings must equal the funded gross to the cent, else the journal is refused with `LDG_RUN_TOTAL_MISMATCH`.
- **Catalogue registration test.** A test enumerates `catalogue.ts` and asserts that every A14 action code resolves in A03's action catalogue and has at least one A14 `approval_policies` row whose `policy_code` meets the row's floor and whose maker and checker roles match the column above and the product map's D07 row; it fails, and the node cannot pass its gate, until A03 and A14 carry every code marked *(A03/A14 to add)*.

**Withdrawal policy (`policy.ts`, read by A39)**

- The thresholds live in one place: A14's immutable `approval_policies` rows for `withdrawal.release` (default: AUTO up to A$25,000.00 inclusive to an allow-listed destination in the investor's verified name; `4E` TRSY → CFO above it; the second-approval row from A$50,000.00 inclusive). The old keys `ledger.withdrawal.auto_max_cents`, `ledger.withdrawal.second_approval_cents` and `ledger.deposit.manual_review_cents` do not exist; A14's seed carries literal cent tiers.
- `evaluateWithdrawalPolicy({ amount_cents, destination_allowlisted, same_day_sum_cents, policy_rows }) → { policy_code, second_approval: boolean, checks: [...] }` is pure over its inputs; `policy_rows` is the snapshot A14's resolver returns, and a thin wrapper `resolveWithdrawalPolicy(investor_id, amount_cents, destination_allowlisted, now)` fetches the rows through A14's `index.ts` and computes `same_day_sum_cents`. `policy_code` is A14's code for the matched row (`AUTO`, `4E`, or the second-approval row's code, expected `6E` TRSY → CFO plus a second RealX checker); this node invents no code of its own. AUTO applies only when `destination_allowlisted` is true and `same_day_sum_cents + amount_cents ≤` the AUTO cap. The checks list carries the design's three items for large withdrawals (`bank_in_name`, `g02_rescreen`, `g14_pattern`) as codes for A39 to run.
- `same_day_sum_cents` is the Σ of this investor's `withdrawal` reservations whose `created_at` falls on the same **Australia/Sydney civil date** as `now` and whose state is `active` or `consumed` (A39's requested, checking, held, awaiting-approval, approved, paying and paid withdrawals; released and expired reservations, i.e. cancelled, rejected or expired withdrawals, do not count), excluding the current request and every reservation created after it (ordered by `created_at`, then reservation id). It is computed once, when A14 resolves the request at submit, through the fact provider `sameDaySumCents(investor_id, reservation_id)` this node publishes for A14's same-day predicate; A14 stores the value in the request's fact snapshot, and binding check 4 replays that snapshot rather than recomputing it. So an A$20,000.00 `AUTO` withdrawal and a later A$10,000.00 `4E` withdrawal on the same day both post, in either order, and neither is re-tiered by the other or by a Sydney midnight or a `policy.change` between approval and posting.
- Boundaries are inclusive at the threshold: A$25,000.00 is still AUTO-eligible, A$25,000.01 is not; A$50,000.00 needs the second approval. The design's copy says "over A$50,000", which reads as exclusive; the product map and A14's row say "≥ A$50k". This node follows the inclusive rule and records the wording difference for A59's copy and for the founder.
- Withdrawals within the AUTO band are not "no approval": they carry an `AUTO` approval id issued by A14's service path, so every `withdrawal.pay` journal has an approval id and the audit row reads the same way at every size.
- Until A14's engine is built this node's unit tests use a fake resolver plus a contract test against A14's published interface; the gate runs the binding oracle and the policy tests against the real A14 engine (A14 is a dependency).

The node QA writes the hidden oracle to `<workspace>\gauntlet\hidden\A16\` first: Vitest suites over the service layer against the Docker Postgres from A01, fast-check generators for accounts, catalogue-valid journal commands, reservations and reversals, approval fixtures created through the real A14 engine, and a fault-injection harness that kills the connection between statements.
- **Build (Grok 4.7 xhigh, split by the planner).** Suggested shape: four parallel sub-agents, then integration. (1) Module schema and chart of accounts: the registered tables (`ledger_reservation`, `ledger_journal_type`, `ledger_approval_draw`, `ledger_approved_file_line`, `ledger_payout`, and `ledger_snapshot_line` if needed) in `modules/ledger/schema.ts` with their migrations, the catalogue seed, `ensureAccount`, the config fragment; nothing under `packages/db` or `packages/config`, and no re-implementation of A04's balance, tenant or insert-only triggers. (2) Posting engine: account locking, catalogue and escrow-leg validation, the five-part approval binding over A14's interface, the approved-file binding and `ledger_payout` routing, origin resolution, balance and reservation maths on A02's `Cents`, reversal, freeze, A10 audit and outbox writes in one transaction. (3) Policy, snapshots and jobs: `evaluateWithdrawalPolicy` and its resolver wrapper, `snapshot`/`rebuild`, `ledger.integrity_nightly`, `ledger.reservation_sweep`. (4) HTTP and contracts: zod schemas with `.meta({ id })`, public read routes with A03 scope ids, internal write routes with service-principal guards, error catalogue, OpenAPI tags, the `me/wallet` and `me/ledger` mappers, fixtures that reproduce the design figures from A06's seed. Integration: the module's own suite (catalogue coverage matrix, 10,000-sequence conservation property, 1,000 parallel reservations, replayed references, approval-binding matrix, boundary tests on every policy threshold), then `npm run verify` and `openapi:check`.
- **Challenge (node QA, Opus 5.5 high).** Runs the hidden oracle, then: 100,000 random journal sequences with reversals and reservations interleaved, asserting Σ balances per side unchanged, zero-sum per currency, the per-order escrow invariant, `available ≥ 0` and `reserved ≤ balance` after every step; fault injection at every statement boundary inside `postJournal`; a webhook replay of every A17-facing type; principal probes (investor, sponsor, staff without the M16 read cell, and an A12 token of every principal type, investor, sponsor, partner and staff) against every route; a journal mixing `operating` with a client account under each catalogue type; frozen-account debit through every debit-bearing type; the approval-binding matrix below; withdrawal amounts at 24,999.99 / 25,000.00 / 25,000.01 and 49,999.99 / 50,000.00, two same-day A$24,999 requests, a destination not on the allow-list, and requests at 23:59:59 and 00:00:00 Australia/Sydney (including both daylight-saving changeover days); a deposit of A$9,999.99 with an exact match (`deposit.matched` posts with no approval) and A$10,000.00 (`deposit.matched` refused with `LDG_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`; `deposit.unmatched` then `suspense.resolve` posts only under a `4E` `deposit.manual_match` approval on `bank_transaction:<id>`); a back-dated journal after a snapshot; `rebuild` after 1,000 snapshots; the `me/wallet`, `ledger/accounts` and `client-money/summary` JSON diffed against the design figures from A06's seed; OpenAPI diffed against the routes; no vendor calls; only the owned path changed. MCP parity for these reads is proven by A100 and A104, not here.
- **Fix loop.** Max 3. Each loop re-runs the full hidden oracle and the failing challenges.
- **Escalate** when the same money invariant fails three times, when the builder proposes relaxing a rounding rule, the zero-sum check or the approval binding to make a test pass, when a fix needs a column in a table A04 owns beyond the A04.1 list or a new function in A02 (spec defect, reopen upstream), when A14's `index.ts` does not expose the approval payload, the submit-time fact snapshot or the replay resolver, or when A03 or A14 lacks a catalogue action code (reopen A03 or A14), or when a catalogue row's approval policy contradicts the product map's maker-checker rows for D07 (ask the user).

## Node-specific challenges (node QA)

- **Crash between legs (money).** The harness drops the database connection after the debit entry insert but before the credit entry insert, and again after the entries but before the audit event: the journal must not exist, the balances must be unchanged, the outbox must be empty, and a retry with the same `external_ref` must post exactly once.
- **Concurrent reservations overdraw (money).** 1,000 parallel `reserve` calls of A$10 against an `investor_cash` account holding A$5,000: exactly 500 succeed, the rest fail with `LDG_RESERVE_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE`, and `reserved` never exceeds `balance` in any intermediate read.
- **Sponsor vehicle money path (money, tenancy).** For an offering and a distribution run of a sponsor vehicle, post `order.fund`, `order.refund` for part of the line, `escrow.release`, `distribution.fund` and `distribution.pay`: every journal posts, every row carries the platform `tenant_id` with the sponsor's `sponsor_tenant_id` and `vehicle_id` as attributes, A04's same-tenant trigger accepts each, the zero-sum holds per currency, and a sponsor session or sponsor token reading the ledger tables or routes sees nothing.
- **Approval binding matrix (compliance, G13).** For each approval-bound row: a valid approval posts; the same approval with the amount one cent higher (`LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`); an A$100 approved withdrawal used to post A$49,000; a `4E` approval on a withdrawal the resolver puts at the second-approval code, and a `4E` approval on `escrow.release`; an approval for another object of the same type; an approval in `queued` (timelock running), `closed`, `reconciled`, `rejected` or `cancelled`; an `approved` approval whose `expires_at` has passed but which the expiry sweep has not yet moved; a `queued_for_custody` approval past its `execution_deadline`; a single-object approval reused on a second journal with a new `external_ref`; an approval whose stored `policy_code` differs from the replay of its own submit-time snapshot. Batch rows: a registered file whose hash differs from the payload's `file_sha256`; `distribution.pay` for a holder not in the file; two lines with A$10.00 moved from one holder to the other at an unchanged total; a line with the same gross but a changed withholding split; the same line posted twice under different `external_ref`s; `distribution.pay` lines whose Σ would exceed the approved gross by one cent; a revert refund with no `offer.revert_and_refund` approval, with another offering's revert approval, and with a refund file whose hash differs from the revert payload's. Every one is refused (`LDG_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` or `LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`) and leaves no row, no audit event and no outbox event.
- **Clocks that must not strand executed approvals (G13).** A `fee.sweep` approved at hour 2, accepted for execution, and confirmed by the bank 80 h after submit posts exactly once; the fifth 200-line chunk of a refund batch, posted 90 h after submit while the approval is `confirmed`, posts; the same approval cannot reach `reconciled` before its last line posts, because `journalPostcondition` is false until then.
- **Resolved at submit, posted later (G13, J15).** Two same-day withdrawals to an allow-listed account: A$20,000.00 (`AUTO`), then A$10,000.00 (`4E`, same-day cap exhausted). Posting the second before the first: both post, and neither is refused. A `4E` withdrawal approved at 23:50 Sydney posts at 00:10 the next day. An `AUTO` withdrawal approved, then an approved `policy.change` that lowers the AUTO cap below its amount, then `withdrawal.pay`: it posts under its stored code and policy version; a new identical request resolves to `4E`.
- **Payouts that come back (money).** An `escrow.release` whose trustee settlement the bank fails: the money returns to the offering's `settlement:<offering id>` line, never to an investor's cash, and `escrow.settlement_resend` sends it again; a failed `withholding.remit` returns to `withholding_payable`; a returned `suspense.return` returns to its `bank_transaction` line; after each, `payout_clearing` equals Σ open payout rows.
- **Shortfall and frozen bank facts (money).** A bank debit with no ledger cause posts `bank.shortfall` and raises Sev1; `shortfall.topup` under a `6E` approval clears it with both sides balanced; the same top-up with a `4E` approval is refused. A `deposit.returned` on a frozen account posts, is flagged `posted_while_frozen`, alerts MLRO and TRSY only, and the investor's reads show `under_review` with no reason.
- **Operating money counted as client money (compliance).** A caller posts `fee.sweep` with the client side balanced but the operating side one cent short, then posts an `adjustment` whose credit leg is `operating`, then re-labels an account row's `is_client_money` directly in SQL: the first two are refused with `LDG_UNBALANCED` and `LDG_CLIENT_MONEY_BOUNDARY`, the third fails the check constraint, and `client-money/summary` never changes by a cent nor increments `client_money_account_count`.
- **Threshold split and boundary (compliance, G13).** An agent token acting for an investor requests A$24,999.00 twice on one Sydney date to an allow-listed account, then A$50,000.00 exactly: the first is `AUTO`, the second resolves to `4E` because the same-day AUTO cap is exhausted, the third resolves to the second-approval code; a `withdrawal.pay` journal submitted for the second with the first's AUTO approval id is refused with `LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`. A request at 00:00:00 Sydney the next day is AUTO again. Then an approved `policy.change` lowers the AUTO cap and the same A$24,999.00 request resolves to `4E` with no code or config change; editing any config value leaves the result unchanged.
- **Fee swept before a refund (money, G11).** Fund an order (A$2,395.92 into escrow), run `fee.sweep` for everything `fee_payable_realx` holds, then miss the soft cap and refund the order: the sweep moves nothing of this order's fee, and the refund posts the full A$2,395.92 back to `investor_cash`.
- **Reversal without a checker (compliance, G13).** The owning service reverses a `deposit.matched` journal with no approval: refused with `LDG_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`; with a `4E` approval on another journal: `LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`; a reversal of a `6E` `escrow.release` with a `4E` approval: `LDG_APPROVAL_MISMATCH`.
- **Agent provenance (D04).** An investor's agent token places an order that funds and requests a withdrawal that pays: the `order.fund` and `withdrawal.pay` journals, their audit rows and the investor's `me/ledger` rows all show the token prefix and the person; a worker post that supplies a different origin than the approval or triggering outbox event is refused with `LDG_ORIGIN_MISMATCH`; an origin with a token and no person is refused with `LDG_ORIGIN_INVALID` before any write.
- **Frozen after approval (compliance, tipping-off).** A withdrawal is reserved and approved, then A42 freezes the account, then A39 posts `withdrawal.pay`: refused with `LDG_ACCOUNT_FROZEN` at posting time; the investor's `me/wallet` shows `account_status: under_review` and no reason in any field, while a distribution line still credits the account.
- **Reversal that manufactures money (money).** Reverse an `order.fund` journal after its escrow line was already released to the trustee, then reverse the reversal: the first must be refused with `LDG_REVERSAL_WOULD_OVERDRAW`, and a valid reversal must be refused a second time with `LDG_ALREADY_REVERSED`; across the whole sequence Σ balances per side is unchanged.
- **Snapshot that cannot be reproduced (money).** A journal with `effective_date` before the cut is posted after the snapshot; the `rebuild` of the snapshot's `as_of_seq` must still equal `accounts_hash`, the journal must carry `posted_after_cut`, and the next snapshot must include it once.
- **Design fidelity (read contracts).** From A06's seed, `me/wallet` for Alex Morgan must return `available_cents = 342018` formatted "A$3,420.18" and `awaiting_investment_cents = 239592` (derived from escrow sub-balances only); `ledger/accounts` must list four rows whose ledger-owned fields equal the design (`Client money · wallets`, `••2201`, `Uninvested investor cash`, 981240410; `Offer escrow`, `••2202`, `Primary offers`, 304888000; `Distributions`, `••8810`, `Trust payouts`, 555903234; `RealX operating`, `••8811`, `Fees (not client money)`, 120433190, status `separate`), with the other three rows `unreconciled` under the fake status provider; and `client-money/summary` must return `client_money_total_cents = 1842031644` (981240410 + 304888000 + 555903234), `client_money_account_count = 3` and `bank_account_count = 4`. A response carrying a float, a `number` money field or an unformatted string fails the contract test.
- **Seed contract A06 must meet for the check above (A06 reopens to match; this node does not edit the seed).** Every seeded ledger row is posted through `postJournal` in this catalogue's shapes, never raw-inserted, so every invariant holds at load; every funded order of an open offering sits in its offering's `offer_escrow` at its full total (fee included) under its own `line_ref` and `beneficiary_id`, Σ over open offerings = 304888000 and equals the escrow `bank_mirror`; fees sit in `fee_payable_realx` only for offerings already released; the three client-money `bank_mirror` balances are the design figures above and the operating mirror is 120433190; Alex Morgan's `investor_cash` available is 342018 and his Kent Street order line is 239592 in escrow. A seed that holds only subtotals in escrow (for example A$3,513,200.00) or puts an open offering's fee in `fee_payable_realx` fails this node's gate by design.

- Policy: no approval threshold exists as a config key or literal in `src/`; boundary tests at 24,999.99 / 25,000.00 / 25,000.01 and 49,999.99 / 50,000.00 cents-exact, the same-day cap across the Sydney midnight and both daylight-saving changeovers, the deposit manual-match boundary at 9,999.99 / 10,000.00, and an approved `policy.change` alters the policy result without a code change while a config edit does not.

## Human gates touched

- None directly; this node runs on the sandbox bank facts A17 will post and never sees real money. HG-CAPITAL (opening the client-money trust accounts at an ADI and funding real client money) and HG-VENDOR (the bank rail) gate any use of this ledger outside the sandbox. Counsel questions recorded for HG-LEGAL rather than encoded as rules: whether investor cash in the wallet is client money under the Corporations Act client-money rules or trust money of the trustee, and what that means for the chart of accounts' `is_client_money` labels and the single platform-tenant ledger; how float interest on the trust account is to be treated (the `interest.sweep` row ships disabled); whether the A$25,000 AUTO and A$50,000 second-approval defaults are acceptable, and whether "over A$50,000" (design) or "at or above" (product map) is meant (the founder chooses; they are A14 policy rows). The A$10,000.00 manual match is a J04 and D07 control, not a reporting rule: AUSTRAC threshold transaction reports cover physical currency only, which RealX does not accept, so they do not apply to NPP or PayID receipts; IFTI and SMR duties are A42's. This node only holds the amount and enforces the manual-match approval.
