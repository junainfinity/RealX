# A85 - Observability and runbooks

**The operator's eyes and the operator's checklists: a full OpenTelemetry pipeline added to the compose stack (collector, traces, metrics, logs, dashboards), traces that carry the request, approval, on-behalf-of, token and job ids so one approval or one agent can be followed from the first click to the chain and the register, infrastructure alert rules that route by severity into the one alerts and SLA-clock service (A13) instead of building a second one, health pages for every adapter and for the chain, and the written runbooks R1-R9 plus six operational runbooks (stuck transaction, break resolution, key rotation drill, break-glass, agent traffic kill switch, telemetry loss) for the operators named in [[Human Gates]]. Runbooks are text and checklists here; A103 runs each one as a drill.**

**Build status:** not started. **Milestone:** AM8 (Journeys, hardening and launch). **Lane:** Quality. **Planner:** Opus 5.5 (high). **Builder:** Grok 4.7 (xhigh). **Node QA:** Opus 5.5 (high, every loop). **Graph QA:** Fable 5.1 (high, at milestone close).
**Estimate:** 8-12 agent-hours (build, challenge and verify loops; two services, ten dashboards, a collector with redaction and conformance, fifteen runbooks with a mutation-tested lint, four stack test suites and up to three fix loops, in line with A48 and A103).

**Depends on:** [[A79 - End-to-end journeys- investor money path]], [[A80 - End-to-end journeys- sponsor and operations]]
**Unlocks:** [[A88 - P1 full regression- 18 P1 journeys, three clean runs, every gate exercised]], [[A103 - Runbook drills R1-R9 executed on the stack]]

**Oracle rule:** not an oracle node (`oracle: no`): nothing here moves money, supply or holdings, and no rule is enforced here that is not already enforced and oracle-tested by its owner (A12, A13, A14, A20, A48, A101). The node QA still writes its Challenge material to `<workspace>\gauntlet\hidden\A85\` before the build starts, from this note alone: the planted telemetry faults (a PII string and a token secret smuggled into a span attribute and a log line, under neutral keys and inside free-text messages, a failed read that could show a false green, a missing `approval_id` on a job span), the runbook-lint mutation set (a runbook step that names a missing operation id, an agent-callable act, a ratifier equal to the actor, an R-series step out of order against A48's data, an MCP tool on the `agents.traffic` release row), the promtool rule test vectors, and the expected outcome of each. The builder never reads that folder. After the gate the cases are published so A88 and A103 re-run them as regression.

This node builds no business behaviour and draws no product screen. It watches what AM1-AM7 built and writes down what a human does when something goes wrong. Dashboards are never the source of truth: the unit register, the client-money ledger and the chain, read through the console screens, are. This plan is not legal advice; every regulator-notification step in a runbook is a counsel question, not a rule.

## Build brief

**1. Scope, placement and what is reused (no duplication)**

- Two owned folders. `infra/observability/` holds the compose fragment, the collector, Prometheus, Alertmanager, Tempo, Loki, the log shipper and Grafana configuration, two small Node 24 services (`obs-exporter` and `alert-bridge`), the alert rules, the attribute conventions and the tests. `docs/runbooks/` holds the runbooks, the routing table and the template. Nothing is added under `services/`, `apps/` or `packages/`; no table, no migration, no `/v1` route.
- Everything else is consumed through public interfaces: A09's OpenTelemetry hooks, span attributes, pino log lines and `/metrics`; A23's compose project `realx`, its profiles and its collector stub; A13's alert catalogue, `emit()`, `resolveBySystem()`, SLA policies and escalation paths (the only alert and clock system in the build); A46's integration rows, job runs and dead letters; A27's `GET /v1/chain/status`; A20's reconciliation runs and breaks; A48's runbook rows (`GET /v1/runbooks`), incidents, drills, halts and kill switches; A12's introspection and agent activity; A101's anomaly holds and kill-switch tiers; A14's approvals; A15's custody sandbox; A10's daily chain verification result; A09's clock endpoint; A46's `GET /v1/jobs` (`next_due_at` and last run per job).
- **Prerequisite amendments to other nodes' specs.** This node needs changes in five other specs. They are amendments to those specs in the plan, made before this node's Specify step; they are not patched here and not left to an unplanned follow-up loop. Specify does not freeze until each amended spec is frozen and its hash recorded:
  - (a) **A13:** the eight observability and platform alert types of §4 added to the catalogue, each with one fixed `owner_role` and fixed secondary roles as listed there (A13: "adding a type is a data change plus a seed test"), and A13's catalogue-count pass criterion updated to include them; internal routes `POST /internal/alerts/emit` and `POST /internal/alerts/resolve` that accept only the `svc-alert-bridge` SVCA principal and only catalogue codes, refuse HUMAN, PARTNER and AGNT principals, call the existing `emit()` and `resolveBySystem()`, and carry A13's own tests.
  - (b) **A23:** its image-lock check also reads `infra/observability/images.lock.json`.
  - (c) **A03:** permission-matrix rows for two new SVCA principals: `svc-observability` holding exactly the read cells (`V`) in §3's read table and no other cell, and `svc-alert-bridge` holding only the execute cell on A13's internal emit and resolve routes.
  - (d) **A04:** `service_account` rows for both, each with a key-custody statement (a sandbox client credential generated on first boot into the `realx_state` volume, never committed; production custody is an HG-PROD decision), caps (`svc-observability` read-only with a request-rate cap; `svc-alert-bridge` an emit-rate cap) and the disclosure flag (the BQA-10 lesson).
  - (e) **A11:** how an SVCA gets a non-interactive credential: a client credential minted into `realx_state` at boot and exchanged for a short-lived service session, with no interactive sign-in, as A11 already requires of SVCA.

  If any amendment is refused, the node escalates rather than working around it.
- Start command: `npm run obs:up` (registered under A01's script convention) runs `docker compose -f infra/stack/compose.yml -f infra/observability/compose.obs.yml` with A23's default profiles plus a new `obs` profile. The overlay adds the observability services and merges into A23's `otel-collector` only two things: the pinned collector-contrib image and the config mount from `infra/observability/collector/`. A23's own `stack:up` without the overlay is unchanged and must still pass A23's checks (stub mode).

**2. Traces and logs that follow one approval, one agent, one job**

- `infra/observability/conventions/attributes.yaml` fixes the span and log attribute names every dashboard and search uses: `realx.request_id`, `realx.trace_id` on log lines, `realx.operation_id`, `realx.principal_type` (`anonymous | HUMAN | PARTNER | AGNT | SVCA`, A11's `principal_type` set), `realx.principal_kind` (A11's sub-kinds under the type, for example `investor`, `sponsor_user`, `staff`), `realx.tenant_id`, `realx.approval_id`, `realx.on_behalf_of_id`, `realx.token_id` (the `TOK-` id, never the secret), `realx.job_name`, `realx.job_run_id`, `realx.outbox_event_id`, `realx.custody_op_id`, `realx.tx_hash`, `realx.recon_run_id`, `realx.incident_id`, `realx.money` (true when the operation carries A09's `x-realx-money`). The names are the ones A09 and the domain modules already emit. For every attribute the file records its source node and its value set. Where an emitter uses a different key, the file records the alias and the collector's transform processor normalises it; for example A09's log field `principal_kind` and A11's session variable `app.principal_kind` carry the four-value type set, so they are aliased to `realx.principal_type`, not to `realx.principal_kind`. An alias renames a key only; an emitter whose values fit neither recorded set is a fix item on that emitter. Where an id is missing (for example a job span without `job_run_id`, or an audit event without a trace id, which the architecture brief requires), that is a fix item on the emitting node; this node never invents the value.
- Collector pipeline (`collector/config.yaml`, contrib distribution, pinned by digest): OTLP in on 4317 and 4318, then `memory_limiter`, then a redaction stage, then a conformance stage, then the `spanmetrics` connector, then export to Tempo (traces), Prometheus (metrics, via remote write) and Loki (logs, via OTLP). Sampling in local mode keeps 100 % of spans. The tail-sampling policy that production would use is written as a separate config file that always keeps spans with `realx.approval_id`, `realx.token_id`, `realx.money = true` or an error status; switching to it is an HG-PROD decision, not something this node does.
- Redaction is the second line behind A09's pino and span redaction, never the first, and it applies to every signal: spans, metrics and logs all enter through the collector's OTLP receiver. By key: a generated file `collector/pii-keys.yaml`, built by a script from `@realx/db`'s exported `PII_PATHS`, drops any attribute whose key matches. By value, on every attribute value and on every log message body whatever its key: a generated file `collector/pii-values.yaml`, built by the same script, holds regex rules for the token secret pattern `rx_[a-z]+_[A-Z2-7]{12}_[A-Z2-7]{32}`, `Bearer ` values, cookies, 64-hex private-key shapes, email addresses, BSBs (`NNN-NNN`), account-number-shaped values (6 to 10 digits) next to a BSB, 8- or 9-digit values that pass the TFN checksum (the checksum is checked in a transform function, so ordinary 9-digit ids are not dropped), and A06's fixture PII strings. A span attribute or log field that matches is dropped; a log message body that matches has the matched value replaced by `[redacted]` and the line kept. Every drop or replacement increments `realx_obs_redacted_total{kind, service}`, which raises `obs.pii_redacted` (§4) because a drop means an upstream emitter leaked; values are dropped, never hashed or kept.
- Conformance: spans of kind server, consumer and job that lack a required attribute for their kind (every server span: `request_id`, `operation_id`, `principal_type`; every span whose `principal_type` is `AGNT`: `token_id` and `on_behalf_of_id`; every job span: `job_name`, `job_run_id`; every approval-bearing operation: `approval_id`) are counted in `realx_obs_span_missing_attr_total{service, kind, attribute}`. The count must be zero after the full A79 and A80 runs.
- Logs: a Grafana Alloy container reads container stdout through the Docker API socket, mounted read-only and only when `RUN_MODE=local` (a verify test refuses the mount under any other run mode), parses A09's JSON lines and forwards them over OTLP to the collector's receiver (`otel-collector:4317`), so every log line passes the same redaction and conformance stages before the collector exports it to Loki. Alloy's config has no Loki writer; the collector is Loki's only writer, and `test/no-bypass.test.ts` checks both the configs (no `loki.write` component or Loki URL outside the collector's exporter) and Loki's ingest (every stream carries the collector's resource attribute). Loki labels are limited to `service`, `level` and `principal_type`. Ids stay in the log body, never as labels. Loki's derived field on `trace_id` links to Tempo; Tempo's trace-to-logs links back.
- Saved searches and a "Follow" dashboard: by `realx.approval_id` (the maker's request, each checker's approval, the custody sandbox signature, the broadcast, the indexer confirmation, the registry acknowledgement and the next reconciliation run all appear, so SM-00's `draft → closed` path can be read on one screen), by `realx.on_behalf_of_id` and `realx.token_id` (everything one person's agents did, with G22 outcomes), by `realx.job_run_id`, by `realx.incident_id`, and by `realx.tx_hash`.
- Access: Grafana, Tempo and Loki are local engineering tools, bound to `127.0.0.1` and reached only from the build machine. The person-level searches (`on_behalf_of_id`, `token_id`) sit outside RBAC, tenant scoping and A10 audit, so they are for engineering only: A12's activity list stays the audited view of what one person's agents did, and no runbook "done when" cell points at a person-level Follow search. Production access (single sign-on, per-role access, audit of person-level trace searches) is an HG-PROD item recorded in each runbook's **Production differences**.

**4. Alert routing by severity into the one SLA-clock service**

- Rules live in `rules/*.yaml` (Prometheus). Every alerting rule carries the labels `severity` (`sev1` to `sev4`), `realx_alert_type` (an A13 catalogue code), `owner_role`, `runbook` (an R or O id from §5) and `dedup_key`. A lint refuses a rule without all five, or with a type code missing from A13's `GET /v1/alerts/types`.
- No double-raising. Signals that a domain module already raises (A27 `chain.stuck_or_lagging`, A46 `integration.degraded` and `job.dead_letter`, A20 `recon.break`, A13 `audit.chain_broken`, A12 and A101 `agent.*`, A48 `kill_switch.engaged` and `incident.declared`) appear on the dashboards but have no alerting rule here; a test fails if any rule's type is in that list. The rules here cover only what no module can see from inside itself, as eight new catalogue types (amendment (a) in §1), each with one fixed owner role (first) and fixed secondary roles: `platform.service_down` (Sev1, ENGO; secondary SECO: api or worker `/readyz` failing for 2 minutes), `platform.api_error_rate` (Sev2, ENGO: 5xx above 2 % of requests over 5 minutes, gate refusals excluded), `platform.latency_slo_burn` (Sev3, ENGO: p95 above 500 ms for reads or 1 s for writes over 15 minutes), `obs.backstop_missing_run` (Sev2, ENGO; secondary the owner roles of the three jobs as A46's catalogue names them, fixed at Specify: `realx_job_overdue` is 1 for the nightly snapshot, reconciliation or sanctions re-screen, the backstop for a scheduler that failed silently), `obs.telemetry_gap` (Sev3, ENGO: no spans from api or worker for 5 minutes while `/readyz` is up; on an idle stack the api still traces `obs-exporter`'s 30-second reads and the worker traces A09's scheduler tick, so idleness is never a gap; if A09 does not trace its scheduler tick, that is a fix item on A09), `obs.pii_redacted` (Sev2, SECO: the collector dropped a PII key or a secret-shaped value), `obs.live_adapter_seen` (Sev1, SECO; secondary ENGO: any integration environment `live`, or a chain id other than 31337 or the testnet id, on a local stack) and `agent.traffic_surge` (Sev3, SECO: platform-wide agent calls above five times the seven-day baseline for 10 minutes and above a floor of 300 calls in those 10 minutes, which A101's per-token rules cannot see; the rule is inactive until seven days of baseline exist, so a fresh stack never raises it).
- Every rule has a promtool rule unit test (`rules/*.test.yaml`) with synthetic series for firing, not firing, the boundary value, the absent-data case and, for the surge rule, the warm-up period, run in the Docker-free `npm run verify`. The live planted signals in the Gauntlet add to these tests; they do not replace them, because a 15-minute latency burn or a seven-day baseline cannot be planted quickly on the stack.
- On the standard seed, the idle stack raises no observability alert: the only open alerts are the seeded ones (`adCmd`: open alerts 3 with 1 high).
- Alertmanager (`alertmanager/config.yaml`) has exactly one receiver, `alert-bridge`, on the internal compose network. There are no email, chat or pager receivers and no external URL; paging is A13's `alert.page` outbox event delivered by A43 to its sandbox channel. Routes by severity: Sev1 `group_wait` 0 s, Sev2 10 s, Sev3 1 minute, Sev4 5 minutes. `platform.service_down` suppresses Sev2 and Sev3 alerts about the same service while it is firing.
- `alert-bridge` (Node 24, owned here): receives Alertmanager's webhook with a bearer secret generated on first boot. It checks each alert's labels against an allow-list and A13's type list; an unknown type or label is refused and counted, never emitted. It calls A13's internal emit route as the service account `svc-alert-bridge` (registered by amendments (c) to (e) in §1), which may emit and resolve only. It is idempotent on the Alertmanager fingerprint and `startsAt`. On `resolved` it calls the resolve route only for types whose `closes_by` is `system`. When A13 is unreachable it retries with backoff from a bounded on-disk queue. The payload carries rule labels, the metric value and the threshold, never a log line or span attribute.
- SLA clocks are A13's, not the bridge's.
